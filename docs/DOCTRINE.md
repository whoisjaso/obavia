## 2. The thirty laws, in one breath each, with where they live

### The call funnel (CF)
1. **Every number is modelled conservatively; cash collected is the only AOV.** `model.md`; every figure carries ASSUMPTION until the scorecard replaces it.
2. **Cost per call = CPM x CTR x click-to-booked; tolerance set by cash per sale.** Cost per dealer application and per first lot, `model.md` §4.
3. **Goal / cash per sale / close rate / show rate = bookings; x cost per call = spend.** Deposits needed per week, `model.md` §3.
4. **Bookings / 22 days / 8 slots = closers; hire ahead.** Lots per desk per week; a second desk at 80 percent for two Mondays.
5. **Friction and intent are one dial.** The GDN number is the first question on the application; the drag, the hold and the deposit are the friction the buyer pays.
6. **One object of attention per page.** The dealer page is headline, film, application. The Lane is one car. The Bid sheet is one number.
7. **Integrate application and scheduler.** The key is issued on the submit screen; the first Let It Go is the "call".
8. **The action flips scanner to justifier; feed data through five vessels.** The deposit and Go confirmations, push and SMS, retargeting above 150 people, the desk person as setter. No AI manipulation.
9. **Offload education into assets; closers take layups.** The Floor screen educates the dealer before Go; the desk person calls the winner within an hour and says the deadline, nothing else.
10. **When it fails, find the most contracted stat and double the easiest.** The Monday scorecard: five numbers, one bottleneck, one action, one date.

### The confirmation page (CP)
1. **Before the action they scan; after it they justify. Educate only after.** Zero reads on the Lane; the four terms and the deposit line come at the first bid; education starts on the confirmations.
2. **The main video is due diligence or urgency, never anything else.**
3. **Originator gives due diligence; vessel gives urgency.** The dealer's main video is who Obavia is and the $0 fee; the buyer's is the clock and the deposit back.
4. **Urgency rests on a third-party, dated, verifiable fact.** The server clock, the holding cost at 0.1 percent a day with its source, the Cox fact stated as US with its date. Never "five slots left".
5. **Do their due diligence for them.** Publish the ten pilot results best, average and worst, the reserve ratio as 1.00, the dispute count, with dates.
6. **One breakout per question the desk is tired of answering.** Six for buyers, six for dealers, under two minutes, titled with the question.
7. **Right topics give 50 percent play and 80 percent engagement; below 80 the content is wrong.**
8. **One correct top video unlocks everything beneath.** The main video above the breakouts.
9. **Say the same thing on page, push, SMS and the desk; then diagnose consumption versus messaging.** The same deadline, the same words, the same Bodoni number everywhere.
10. **Testimonials are the least believed asset; answer "how bad could it get".** No testimonials, anywhere, ever. Two worst-case breakouts.

### The ad strategies (AD)
1. **Standard events, never custom conversions.** Lead, CompleteRegistration, AddPaymentInfo, Purchase at the engine start, sent server side.
2. **Optimize one step deeper only above 50 a week per ad set.** Not inside the twelve weeks.
3. **Mark the qualified event through CAPI.** `startedAt` is the Purchase.
4. **Stack low-overlap events.** Phase 5, never at test budgets.
5. **Messaging first, then the event; the pixel third.** One change per week per funnel.
6. **Wrong people arriving is a messaging failure.** Rewrite the pair, never the audience.
7. **Direct response is the cheapest default.** The 15-second ad to the lane link to the deposit.
8. **Content before the ask only when direct response is too cold.** Phase 5, by the numbers, never by taste.
9. **A 3-second view makes a stranger retargetable.** The audience is created on day one and not served until 150.
10. **Fifteen to twenty unique pieces in the window, never one piece repeated.** Each push a different pair; the same push never twice.

## 3. The owner's angles, in one breath each

- **Lego** (`docs/design/lego.md`). Two pieces placed next to each other, no connector; the user joins them and feels clever. 97 percent visual on scanner surfaces; bold-only lines on justification surfaces; the body (haptic, sound, motion within 100 ms) is the confirmation, never a sentence. §8 there binds each law above to a placement.
- **One action** (`docs/plan/one-action.md`). Every screen has one thing to do, it is obvious, it outranks everything, and nothing not yet relevant is on the glass. A screen with two primaries is two screens.
- **Clouds** (`docs/design/cloud-system.md`, `docs/design/blender-pipeline.md`). Everything soft, lit from above, translucent at the edge, never still. Buttons are rendered volumetric clouds that sink, rise, shed wisps and breathe; the label is carved into them; the sounds are air in one room. Blender is the asset source; the live engine and the sprites come from the same material.
- **Native** (`docs/design/native-patterns.md`). Only borrowed gestures (Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, Marketplace, iOS). A gesture that needs a tooltip is rejected. Every hover, press and release has a visible aftermath.
- **Legibility** (§4 below, the owner, October 13, 2026). The people who use this are not technical. Beauty never costs a dealer or a buyer the ability to do the one thing without being told.
- **Accountability.** The growth plan is rigid; a deviation is named in the first line of the reply with the plan line it conflicts with, and is not built until the changelog carries a dated amendment. Missed gates are reported with the number and the days late.
- **Claims.** Everything on the glass matches verified capability. No fee of any kind before Obavia's own GDN (43 TAC §215.85). No outcome claims, no savings claims, no "better than" anyone as text. Facts carry a source and a date.

## 4. Legibility for people who are not technical

The owner, October 13, 2026: "Still make this completely legible. A lot of the people that will use it are non-technical and they need an easy application, both for dealers and individuals. A proper UI that makes sense relationally."

Rules, each with a test:

1. **The one verb is a word a dealer's desk person already uses.** Let it go, Go, Sold, Paid, Scan, Start. Bid, Pay, That's me, Done. Never a system word (submit, confirm, authorize, sync). Test: read the button aloud at a lot; nobody asks what it means.
2. **The thing and its meaning touch.** A number sits on the thing it measures; a step sits where the last one ended; the pass's six sit under its code. The Lego placements (§4 of lego.md) are the grammar of legibility, not decoration. Test: cover the words; the layout alone says what goes with what.
3. **Type is big and upright.** Bodoni for money and names at the price size; platform face for the rest, never under 17 px on the glass; upright spaced capitals on every primary. No italics, no grey-on-grey, contrast at or above 4.5:1 measured from the pixels, the carved label included.
4. **One screen, one thing, one tap.** No menus to learn, no settings to find, no modes. The next thing appears where the last one ended. Test: a first-time buyer wins a car and reaches Plates without a single "where do I go".
5. **Pictures of the real thing.** The objects a dealer and a buyer already know, rendered in the house material (§5): the car, the key, the title, the plate, the pass, the clock. A pictogram the user has to decode is a tooltip in disguise and fails the native test.
6. **The body says it happened.** The press sinks, the cloud lands, the haptic ticks, the sound breathes. No toast, no "Success", no spinner where a swell will do.
7. **Silence on negative paths.** A wrong thing just does not happen; the stamp or the faint line says why in four words or fewer.
8. **The phone is the test device.** 393 wide, one thumb, in daylight on a lot. If it needs a desktop or two hands, it is wrong.
9. **No reading to act.** At most three percent of a scanner surface is text that must be read; legal sentences sit where the statute puts them, in full, and nothing is added around them.
10. **The stranger test, every hand-back.** Hand the screen to someone who has never seen it and give them no words. If they do the one thing in under ten seconds, it passes. If not, the screen is wrong, not the person.

The owner, October 13, 2026, later the same day: "I still want it to be a lot simpler. Have this mindset that people are extremely stupid, and most people aren't even at a sixth grade reading level. We have to make things so simple and so native. We have to make the Lego pieces so close together that we suggest them doing something, and putting the pieces together is their idea. Page by page, one at a time. Lower the text. Way easier to use than Copart. Immediate dopamine rush from specific things that you're doing. You automatically know where things are. It's obvious."

11. **Write for a ten-year-old, then cut half.** Every word on the glass is one a ten-year-old says; no word over two syllables unless it is the car's own name or the law's own word; no sentence where a number and a noun will do. Test: read the screen aloud to a child; they can say what to do next.
12. **One thing per page, and the page is the thing.** When a screen holds two things, it is two screens. A choice is one tap among at most three pieces; a fact is one line; a field is one page with the keyboard already up. Test: count the things a stranger must look at before the verb; more than two is a split.
13. **The next piece touches, so the step is their idea.** Nothing tells the person what to do next; the next piece sits touching the last one, lit, where the thumb already is, and the person reaches for it. No arrow, no hint, no "tap here". Test: cover every word; the eye still lands on the next piece.
14. **Immediate reward, every time, on every good tap.** Within 100 ms of a good tap the body gets all three at once: the cloud moves, the sound breathes, the haptic ticks; the big moments (the bid landing, the win, the deposit back, the car started) get the rendered moment and the Bodoni number landing. Nothing good waits for a sentence; nothing bad gets a sound. Test: tap with the sound off and the eyes closed; the thumb still knows it worked.
15. **Fewer words every pass, never more.** The walkthrough's reads per screen only go down; the ceiling is three words to act, the kept legal reads aside, and a screen over it is a screen to split. Test: `tools/walkthrough/run.sh --strict` passes.

## 6. The audit every agent runs before handing back

Write these lines into the report, filled in:

- Law obeyed: CF / CP / AD number, or "none applies, because ...".
- One action: the screen's one verb, and what is hidden until relevant.
- Lego: the two pieces and their placement; the inference the user makes; the body proof.
- Legibility: the verb read aloud; the stranger test result; the measured contrast.
- Clouds: the material, the press physics, the sound, the aftermath on hover and click.
- Claims: every fact's source and date; nothing promised that is not built.
- Deviation: none, or the plan line it conflicts with and the changelog date.

A hand-back without these lines is not finished.
