# Obavia sales doctrine

This is how Obavia sells, and it is the frame the product coaches our clients' teams against. It is distilled from the script Andres teaches (the one behind reps going from $4K to $65K and from $15K to $70K a month in commissions), and adapted to Obavia.

Read with `docs/obavia-spec.md` (what we build) and `docs/icp-language.md` (how the market talks).

---

## 1. The string

A sale is a string from the first time they see you to the money. Every step on it has to be hit, whoever hits it: the setter, the closer, an ad or a video.

1. Problem aware
2. Solution aware
3. Brand aware
4. Most aware, and aware of the gap between where they are and where they want to be
5. Paid

If the setter skips a step, the closer has to go back and hit it. **Obavia tracks the string on every lead**, so the closer knows exactly where to pick up, and the owner can see which setter keeps skipping steps.

## 2. The homework: know the problem you solve

Before any call, know what problem you solve for this ICP and what it causes in their life. Then you never guess with a question. You ask knowing the answer, and you steer them to say it.

## 3. The set (setter, or the first 15 minutes of a closer's call)

| Step | What you ask | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Intent** | Open like you know them: "Hey John," never "Is this John?" Reconfirm why they came in. | Familiarity. |
| **Goal** | "What was the main goal, even downloading that?" | **Whatever they want is what they don't have.** The goal is the problem, stated positively. |
| **The approach** | "What are you doing about it now? How long? What made you choose that?" | Put one **label** on everything they do ("that approach"), so it can be dropped as one thing later. |
| **Catalyst** | "It's not a bad strategy. What changed that made you want more advanced help?" | People don't change for no reason. The catalyst is the moment they decided things had to change. It is the real problem. |
| **Pain** | "What do you mean by that? How long has it been going on? Has it had an impact on...?" | Probe for details, time and impact, until the current approach is something they want to walk away from. |
| **Gap (qualify)** | "In a perfect world, six months out, where would you want to be? How far are you from that now?" For savings: "How much would you want saved at that point? Where are you now?" | The size of the gap is the size of the problem and the qualification. |

A perfect set ends here. If the closer is weak, the setter goes further (future pace, consequence) so every lead arrives as primed as possible.

## 4. The close

**Open.** Confirm the setter's notes in the lead's own words: "John mentioned you're doing X, and it's causing Y. Is that about right?" Then: "Did you get a chance to go through the video John sent? What did you take from it?" Saying the takeaway out loud tells them they have already learned something from you.

**Pre-handle the two objections that would come at the end.**

1. **Do it yourself.** "Besides the energy thing, why look at more advanced help instead of just fixing your own routine, like most people do?" Then make them double down: "Why do you feel it hasn't worked when you tried on your own?"
2. **The objection they were planning to give.** Walk the tree:
   - **Were you looking for help before we spoke?**
     - **No** → "What stopped you?" (time, money, partner) → "What's shifted now?" Once they say what changed, that objection is gone.
     - **Yes** → **Did you move forward with something?**
       - **No** → "What prevented you?" → "What changed now?"
       - **Yes, good result** → "What made you want a better result? Why are we speaking?"
       - **Yes, bad result** → "What are you looking for this time, to know it would work?" Their criteria become the pitch: *last time it was X; we don't do X, we do Y.*

**Future pace.** Use an analogy that makes the next answer feel natural: Usain Bolt runs fast toward the medal, and faster with a lion behind him. The pull is the goal, the push is the consequence. Then: "If there was a way to get you there, what would be different?" Take each goal through three layers:

1. **Concept.** "I'd be more productive."
2. **Details.** "Give me an example. Why does that matter to you?"
3. **Imagery.** "Step into those shoes. What would that actually mean for you, personally?" Never ask "how would you feel". You want them to feel it, not name it.

Get two or three goals.

**Consequence.** Recap the analogy and go to the other side: "What happens if the problem continues for two weeks, two months, two years? Between you and I, what would tangibly happen?" Take it through the same three layers. For the imagery, take it off their identity: *"I know this isn't you. You're obviously committed. But who would that person be?"*

**The three commitments.** The gap between the goal and the consequence is urgency. Point it at three commitments:

1. **Do it.** "Are you okay with that happening?"
2. **Now.** "Why now, and not next Monday?"
3. **Them.** "Whose responsibility is it?"

**Transition.** "Based on what you said about X and Y, I think we can help. Would it be appropriate to walk you through how?"

**The pitch: three pillars, short.** Energy drains the longer you talk, so keep it compressed. Each pillar follows one pattern: *The first pillar is X, because you mentioned [their problem], and the way X helps is [...]. Does that feel like it would help?* Use three pillars (four for a complex offer). Accountability always goes third, never first.

**Recommit.** Ask three things:
- "Do you feel this would get you to [goal]?"
- "Out of everything, what's the key to the castle for you?"
- "How would it help you specifically?"

This brings them back to an emotional high before the money.

**Ask.** "The total investment to get to [goal] is $X. How would you like to proceed?" Then handle objections.

## 5. The script is not the difference

Top reps don't follow the script ten times better. They understand the building blocks underneath: how people decide, how they perceive influence, what they deem significant. That is Obavia's edge. The word-choice read (I vs we, my vs our, the one name they reach for) feeds every step of this script.

## 6. How Obavia builds the doctrine into the product

### The handoff (setter → closer), written automatically

Andres' closers open with "I have a couple of notes here from John." Setters write those notes by hand, when they write them at all. Obavia writes them from the call, as a **profile of the lead**. Every closer gets it before the call:

- **Wants:** the goal in their words, and the problem it implies.
- **Their approach:** what they do now, and the label the setter used for it.
- **Catalyst:** what changed that made them reach out, and when.
- **Pain:** what, since when, and its impact.
- **The gap:** where they are now against where they want to be, in numbers. This is the qualification.
- **Identity:** the label the setter gave them ("the closer the team waits on"), used back to them in the close.
- **Archetype:** the probable significance type from their word choice, and what they deem significant.
- **Already handled:** the do-it-yourself answer, the objection-tree path and what changed, and the criteria from a past bad result.
- **Pre-call asset:** whether they watched it, and their takeaway.
- **The string:** which steps were hit and which are missing, so the closer knows where to pick up.
- **For the close:** three crucial points that decide the purchase:
  - how to open (their words, confirmed);
  - the pillar to lead with (the key to the castle, answering why the last attempt failed);
  - the consequence to paint.
  Plus the exact words to use back to them.

A weak set shows up here as missing steps, with a plain instruction to the closer ("the catalyst isn't uncovered; find it before the pitch"). Over time it becomes the setter's own fix.

### Coaching against the doctrine

- **String coverage per rep:** the steps a setter skips most, from their calls. This joins the leak engine as a reason ("set skipped the catalyst on 62% of calls").
- **Talk-time in the pitch:** pitches that run long, measured against the close rate.
- **Pre-handling:** calls where do-it-yourself or the planned objection came up at the end are flagged as "not pre-handled".
- **Commitments:** closes attempted without the three commitments.

### Guardrails

- We amplify pain that is real, in their words. We never invent it.
- The read is probable, never certain, and it is about language, never identity or protected traits.
- No fake urgency and no fake scarcity. A "no" after the three commitments is a real no.

## 7. Obavia's own script (how we sell Obavia)

Our ICP: agency owners at $100K to $1M a month with a team on the phones.

- **Homework: the problem we solve.** The owner can see where deals are lost, but not why. Reps don't log anything, coaching is opinion, sales blames marketing, and the owner ends up closing the big calls themselves.
- **Goal.** "What made you want to look at this?" → usually "more predictable", "the team closing without me", or "scale to X".
- **The approach.** Label what they do now: "the Friday-review approach", "the dashboard approach", "the jump-in approach".
- **Catalyst.** "Reviewing calls on Fridays isn't a bad approach. What changed?" → a lost big deal, a rep who washed out, a bad month blamed on the leads.
- **Pain.** How long, what it has cost in dollars, and whether they can take a week off.
- **Gap.** Revenue now against the target, closers now against closers needed. The closer math on the site does this in public.
- **Do it yourself.** "Why not build the dashboard yourself?" This is our build-vs-buy argument, in their mouth: the dashboard was never the problem, the why was.
- **Objection tree.** They have usually tried Gong, a sales manager, or a coach. Bad result? Their criteria become our pitch: "nobody used it" → "nothing to type"; "generic" → "built from your own calls".
- **Future pace.** The team hitting like their best rep, and the owner off the calls.
- **Consequence.** Another quarter where the owner is the one who has to step up and save it.
- **Three pillars.**
  1. **Why, from every call.** You mentioned you can't tell why deals are lost. Obavia hears every call and tags the reason in the lead's own words.
  2. **The read on every lead.** You mentioned your closers go in cold. Every closer gets the handoff: who this person is, what they deem significant, and the three points that close them.
  3. **The fix, checked Friday.** You mentioned coaching doesn't stick. One fix per rep, checked against their own calls, in dollars. (Accountability goes third.)
- **Ask.** "It's $3,000 a month to know why every deal is lost and fix it by Friday. How would you like to proceed?"

## 8. The offer (the part the script can't fix)

A script converts demand the offer creates. When ads, funnel and sales all look broken at once, the offer is usually the cause. Source: `docs/icp-language.md` §7.

**Match the message to the awareness stage.** Every lead sits somewhere between lost and most aware:

| Stage | Sell | Obavia example |
|---|---|---|
| Lost (unaware) | The goal, made vivid (the dream house) | "What would your month look like if every rep closed like your best one?" |
| Problem aware | The pain, named exactly (the burning building) | "Half your booked calls never show. Here's why." |
| Solution aware | Why us over the others they're comparing | "Dashboards show where. We show why, and fix it by Friday." |
| Product aware / most aware | The decision | Terms, start date, the founding seat |

In the set, the goal question ("what made you want to look at this?") tells you the stage. The handoff carries it to the closer.

**Every objection is an offer change waiting to happen.** Log it, count it, price it, then change the offer so it never comes up again:
- **Budget:** change the terms before the price.
- **Trust or authority:** a refundable start. Meet the team, bring the partner, get a full refund if it isn't right.
- **"Sounds great if it works":** a guarantee on the outcome they named, with simple conditions.
- **"I need X first":** add X. Each objection removed opens a colder audience.

**Front end sells what they want. The back end delivers what they need.** Earn trust with a result before offering more. Never pitch an upsell to a skeptical new client.

**Guardrails.** Guarantees and refunds are promises: only offer what we will honour, in writing, with plain conditions. No fake deadlines, no fake scarcity. The founding cap is real.

### Obavia's own offer (draft, needs the owner's decision)

- **Front end:** the leak found and the first fix assigned in the first two weeks, from the team's own calls.
- **Terms:** monthly, not annual up front. Budget objections get terms, never a discount.
- **Refundable start:** the first two weeks, with a full refund if the first read on their calls isn't worth it.
- **Guarantee (to decide):** for example, "we find at least $X a month of leak in your first 30 days, or you don't pay." Conditions: tools connected and calls recorded.
- **Objection-driven additions:**
  - "We'd have to set it up": we connect every tool for them.
  - "My reps won't use it": nothing to type, and the rep app pays them back in their own number.
- **Back end, after trust:**
  - the handoff and trial mode for their setters;
  - offer analysis from their own objections;
  - the daily report for their marketer.
