# Push and SMS: Expo limits and the rules for a transactional text to a buyer

Researcher-architect, checked 2026-10-12. This file supports the PUSH placement in `growth/content.md`, AD10 and `messaging.md` (two campaigns).

## Question

What are Expo's push limits (rate, payload, batch, receipts)? What must a text to a buyer who gave a number in the app follow on consent wording and STOP handling?

## Answer

Expo accepts up to **600 notifications per second per project** and up to **100 messages per request**. The payload, including the payload sent to iOS and Android, must be **4096 bytes or less**. Push receipts are deleted **24 hours** after sending. The service has **no SLA**. That supports decisions §2 row 7: push is never the only channel for a state change, and the in-app stream carries the truth. For texts, the federal rule (47 CFR §64.1200(a)(10)) says a person revokes consent by any reasonable means. The words **"stop," "quit," "end," "revoke," "opt out," "cancel," "unsubscribe"** revoke consent per se, other wording counts if a reasonable person would read it as revocation, and a revocation must be honoured **within ten business days**. A sender may not name an exclusive way to revoke. The part of the rule that would make one STOP end texts on unrelated topics ("revoke-all") has been delayed. Law-firm reports put the new date at **January 31, 2027**, which is secondary-sourced. CTIA's Messaging Principles (May 2023) treat a text the consumer asked for, about a specific purpose, as **informational**. That category needs consent for that purpose, given with the number, and a call-to-action naming the program, the sending number, the sender, fees, and how to opt out. A recurring campaign needs a confirmation text with frequency, HELP and STOP instructions. Each opt-out gets one final confirmation, then silence. A text that adds a promotion becomes **promotional** and needs written consent. Texas SB 140 (effective September 1, 2025) extends the state's telephone-solicitation registration to texts that try to induce a purchase. Secondary sources report a Secretary of State position that texts sent with prior consent do not need registration. Both points are **UNVERIFIED** against the enrolled bill and stay with counsel.

## The decisions it forces

1. **The phone-number field is the consent.** Under it, in the same view, one line: "Obavia will text you about this sale only: your pay deadline, pickup and plates, from [number]. Message and data rates may apply. Reply STOP to stop, HELP for help." Store the seven CTIA consent fields with the number. This is the customer-care (informational) campaign in `messaging.md`.
2. **A link to a content piece is informational only when it is about that buyer's own sale step**, which `content.md` PUSH already requires. Anything else, such as a breakout about another topic or any "new lots" text, is promotional. It needs a separate written opt-in and its own campaign. This is Texas Chapter 302 territory, so counsel answers before the first send.
3. **STOP handling:** match the seven words case-insensitively, plus a reasonable-person check on any other reply. Write the opt-out within the same minute (the federal ceiling is ten business days, which is no reason to wait). Send one confirmation, then nothing on that campaign. Until January 31, 2027, a STOP on the marketing campaign does not by rule end the sale texts. Obavia ends both anyway, to be safe ahead of the revoke-all date, and keeps the sale's pay deadline in-app and with the desk call (CF9).
4. **Push** sends in batches of ≤100, under 600 a second, with payloads ≤4 KB. Receipts are checked between 15 minutes and 24 hours after sending, and tokens that return `DeviceNotRegistered` are dropped. No business state depends on a push arriving, which matches decisions §2 row 7.
