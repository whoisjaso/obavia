# The public auction model: a licensed dealer's car, an individual buyer, a phone

Researcher-architect. Read 2026-10-12 and 2026-10-13 (each evidence row carries its own date). Builds on `obavia-auction-decisions.md` §0, §5, §8 and `research/texas-rules.md`; nothing already decided there is reopened. Observations are quotes with a URL and date. Inferences are labelled. Anything that could not be read from a primary source is marked **UNVERIFIED**. Nothing below says a thing is lawful without the section that says so.

## Summary

1. Texas has no "public auction licence". A public motor vehicle auction is an Independent GDN holder bound by 43 TAC §215.144(h) and (i) and Transp. Code §503.039; the Wholesale Motor Vehicle Auction GDN is dealer-to-dealer only.
2. Two statutes box the plan: §503.039(a) (title must pass to the selling dealer before the buyer) and §215.144(i)(3) (a GDN holder may not sell another GDN holder's car at a public auction). Together they make "Obavia's GDN sells Triple J's car" a title-assigned purchase, not a consignment.
3. A third rule reaches further: §215.133(a) requires a GDN "for a consignment location". A car sitting on another dealer's lot under Obavia's consignment is a second location for Obavia. Counsel question, not yet in the plan.
4. The statute's only exemption from the GDN for a third party running a sale is a **licensed auctioneer as bid caller** (§503.024(d)); the auctioneer law exempts internet auctions (§1802.002(a)(14)) so an online-only Obavia is neither licensed nor exempt under 503.024(d). Counsel question 12.
5. Money: the selling dealer collects the 6.25% tax and remits with the title application (§152.0411); SPV does not apply when a licensed dealer sells (§152.0412(d)(1), Comptroller 96-254); the $10 metal-plate fee replaced the $5 tag fee on 7/1/2025 (TxDMV HB 718 FAQ).
6. Paperwork is already electronic inside TxDMV: the webDEALER Buyer Acknowledgment replaces the buyer's signature on the 130-U and the odometer disclosure, but only for a buyer with a Texas driver licence or ID. Out-of-state buyers sign paper.
7. A public auction GDN holder must transfer title "before the 21st day after the date of the sale" (§503.039(b)); the 30-day rule in §215.144(f)(3) is not the binding one on an Obavia-GDN lane.
8. Consumer law: the FTC Used Car Rule applies at public auctions to "dealers and the auction company"; DTPA waivers are void without the buyer's own lawyer (§17.42); arbitration is enforceable under the FAA (In re FirstMerit Bank, DTPA claims compelled); no cooling-off exists for a sale at the dealer's place of business (16 CFR 429.0 definition).
9. No rule found requires a bidder's physical presence, a reserve disclosure beyond Bus. & Com. Code §2.328, or a physical auction location for an online lane; §2.328(d) makes undisclosed seller bidding voidable by the buyer.
10. Comparables: BaT and Cars & Bids charge the buyer 5% ($250 min, $7,500 cap) and act as introducers; Copart sends Texas clean-title public buyers to a broker; ACV, ADESA and CarMax are dealer lanes; Facebook removed dealer vehicle listings from business pages on 2023-01-30. Obavia's mechanics match BaT; its legal seat does not.
11. Thirteen decisions and fourteen counsel questions are in §7, formatted for `obavia-auction-decisions.md`.
12. Next action: send §7's counsel questions 11 to 14 with the Phase 1 engagement (decisions §5) by Friday 2026-10-17; question 12 (the licensed-auctioneer path) decides whether Phase 3 needs a GDN at all.

### Inferences (labelled)

- INFERENCE 1.A. §215.144(i)(3) and §503.039(a) read together mean the only way an Obavia-GDN lane sells a Triple J car is after Triple J assigns title to Obavia (a dealer-to-dealer sale, exempt from tax under §152.001(2)(B), row 2.4) and Obavia resells to the buyer. That is structure (b'), not consignment (b). Decisions §0 step 2 ("other dealers join under it") should be read as (b').
- INFERENCE 1.B. Under (b') Obavia is the dealer on every document and carries the title duty on a 21-day clock (1.6(b)), the dealer bond (4.7), VIT on inventory it holds (counsel q2 already open), and the DTPA exposure of a seller (4.2). "Obavia never carries a risk it is not paid for" then prices the seller-side fee against that.
- INFERENCE 1.C. §215.133(a) ("a GDN for a consignment location") was written for cars physically consigned at a place. Whether a car that never leaves Triple J's lot but is offered on Obavia's lane is a "consignment location" for Obavia is unsettled on the text. Under (b') the question changes: the car is Obavia's inventory sitting at another dealer's licensed premises, which §503.038(a)(5) and (11) (plates on cars not "in stock", use of a location "to avoid the requirements") make a cancellation risk. Counsel question 13.
- INFERENCE 1.D. §503.024(d) is a statutory, not a rule-based, exemption. If Obavia (or a named employee) held a TDLR auctioneer licence and acted as bid caller, with title never passing to Obavia and the car at the dealer's licensed lot, the statute says that person "is not engaging in business as a dealer". The open points are (i) whether an online lane with a licensed auctioneer calling it is "conducted at the location for which the general distinguishing number was issued" (the car is; the bidders are not), and (ii) whether TxDMV reads its own §215.85 to apply regardless. Counsel question 12. If it holds, Obavia's fee is an auctioneer's commission under a TDLR contract, no GDN, no VIT, no title duty, and the dealer stays seller of record on every document. This is the only path the research found to a per-car fee without a GDN.
- INFERENCE 1.E. For the Phase 4 individuals' lane, §503.024(b)(1)(B) lets an individual sell "to any person if the sale or offer is made through a licensed auctioneer". The same auctioneer licence that answers 1.D also licenses "Sell it back here" without consigning the car to a dealer. The 1802.002(a)(14) internet exemption cuts the other way: an online sale is exempt from the auctioneer law, so the question is whether a TDLR-licensed person running it still counts as "through a licensed auctioneer". Counsel question 12 covers it.

### Inferences

- INFERENCE 2.A. Deposit custody: nothing in ch. 503, ch. 215 or ch. 152 names who may hold a refundable bid deposit. A refundable deposit is not a "fee … for arranging" under §215.85(a) while it is refundable; a forfeited deposit paid to the platform would be (decisions §1 already routes forfeiture to the dealer). Counsel q3 stands.
- INFERENCE 2.B. Purchase funds: under (a) the dealer must be the payee of price and tax (2.2); a platform that receives the price "acting for the seller" inherits §152.063 records duties (1.17) and is, on §215.85(a)'s words, arranging the transaction. Under (b') Obavia is the seller and the payee. The plan's Phase 1 rule (balance paid to the dealer, Obavia touches only the deposit) is the one consistent with (a).
- INFERENCE 2.C. A platform fee charged to the dealer is a brokerage "for a fee" under §215.85(a) (already decided). A buyer-side premium is also taxable consideration (2.1), as decisions §1 says.
- INFERENCE 2.D. The Desk's out-the-door total should carry the $10 plate fee (2.9), not $5, and may carry up to $20 under §503.0631(f) only if the dealer actually charges it.
- INFERENCE 2.E. Cash sales need no OCCC licence (2.11); the Phase 6 "Finance" word would.

- The 20-working-day statute versus the Comptroller's 30-calendar-day instruction (2.3): which the county enforces is **UNVERIFIED**. The Desk should use the earlier date.
- Whether a county will accept the $10 plate fee through webDEALER for an out-of-state buyer in every county: FAQ says yes (2.9); not tested.

INFERENCE 6.A. Obavia's mechanics are BaT's (timed online, reserve the seller may lower, two-minute extension, card hold or deposit, seller pays a flat amount, buyer pays nothing in Obavia's case). Obavia's legal seat is not BaT's: BaT introduces and steps out; Obavia runs the paperwork and the pickup. In Texas that difference is the whole §215.85 question. Among licensed Texas structures Obavia most resembles a public auto auction under an Independent GDN (1.4) if it takes title, or a licensed auctioneer bid-calling at the dealer's lot (1.10) if it does not. It resembles Copart least: Copart owns the yard and the buyer never meets the consignor.

INFERENCE 6.B. The 5% / $250 / $7,500 buyer fee is the online-auction norm (BaT, Cars & Bids). Decisions §1 chose zero buyer premium; this research adds that in Texas the premium would be taxable consideration (2.1) and that neither BaT's nor Cars & Bids' Texas licensing posture could be verified, so their structure is not evidence that a buyer-side fee escapes §215.85.
