# Cox × Fullpath and DealerCenter: what they do, where they break, where Obavia wins

Researched October 2, 2026, using these sources:
- fullpath.com: home, Equity and Agentic CRM pages;
- Cox Automotive's acquisition releases (April 23 and June 1, 2026) and its media kit;
- dealercenter.com: Pricing and Buy Here Pay Here pages;
- Capterra (DealerCenter, 1,485 reviews);
- the BBB file for Nowcom;
- DealerRefresh threads;
- Ringlead's Fullpath and VinSolutions reviews (a competitor's blog, so treat its framing with care, though its quotes are attributed);
- TIADA on Texas deferred sales tax;
- Reddit, through search excerpts only. Reddit blocks the crawler, so Reddit lines are single comments, not verified facts.

Quotes are what dealers or the vendors wrote. Nothing here comes from inside either company.

## 1. Fullpath, now "Fullpath by Cox Automotive"

**The deal**
- Cox signed on April 23, 2026 and closed on June 1, 2026.
- Israeli press put the price at "hundreds of millions", and ynet said about $500M.
- Fullpath reports to Lori Wittman, Cox's President of Retail Solutions. Founders and service levels stay "for existing clients".

**What it is**
- A **customer data platform** (one profile per shopper, assembled from the DMS, CRM, website and ad data).
- **Marketing automation** on top of the profiles: email, SMS, ads that follow inventory, VIN-specific campaigns, equity campaigns and "dynamic payments" in ads.
- An **"Agentic CRM"** with AI agents that qualify leads, answer phones and create tasks. As of mid-2026 this is a sign-up "implementation list", not a shipped product.

**What it claims**
- ~2,500 dealer clients, 200+ integrations, 11M data points a day and "250K attributed annual sales".

**What it costs (third-party figures)**
- About $1,000 a month for the base platform and about $5,000 with activation. A Fullpath sales manager gave both numbers on DealerRefresh.
- Aggregators say $2,000 to $15,000 a month.

**Who it's for**
- Franchise stores and large independents with real digital ad budgets.
- The buyer is a marketing director.
- Its own case-study names are Findlay, John Elway, Quirk and Berge.

**The Cox thesis in one line**
- Cox owns where shoppers browse (Autotrader, Kelley Blue Book, Dealer.com) and where dealers work (VinSolutions, Dealertrack, vAuto, Xtime).
- Fullpath is the layer that joins them, so a dealer can target "people who looked at this car on Autotrader".
- Cox's own FAQ calls this "something no competitor can replicate".

**Equity, the feature worth studying**
- Every day it computes each customer's equity: book value (from Kelley Blue Book, using service mileage when it has it) minus the loan balance.
- It moves customers into "likely to trade" audiences automatically.
- It sends a 1:1 offer that lands on a page with the same vehicle and valuation the customer saw.

### Where it breaks
- **It is marketing, not operations.** "Marketing/data-focused (no native CRM, voice AI, or service tools yet)." It doesn't take a payment, run a sale or count a drawer.
- **The CRM and voice agent are a waitlist.** The CRM page reads "Join the Implementation List."
- **Garbage in, garbage out.** It "requires clean data inputs." A buy here pay here store's data lives in Frazer or DealerCenter, often messy.
- **Small stores are priced out.** It's built for stores with ad budgets. A 2-to-10-person lot is not the customer.
- **Its independence is gone.** Dealers now ask how Cox will re-price it next to VinSolutions and Dealer.com, and who owns the data.
- **The review base is thin.** G2 lists 20 reviews; the "4.2/5" figure that circulates comes from a small sample nobody can verify.
- **The Cox family's interfaces are disliked.** These quotes are about VinSolutions, not Fullpath:
  - "Layers upon layers of updates upon updates that don't even fully integrate with prior versions." (Dan Sayer, DealerRefresh)
  - "It takes minimum 5 clicks to complete a simple task." (G2)
  - "VinSolutions is like dusting off an original computer and using DOS." (Reddit)
  - "If he [our account manager] leaves, we leave." (Dan Sayer)
  - Mobile reviewers: "Always crashing, always down."

## 2. DealerCenter

**Who owns it**
- Nowcom Corporation runs it.
- Nowcom is **Westlake Financial's sister company under the Hankey group**, and Westlake is a large subprime auto lender (Digital Dealer; Westlake's own site).
- BBB lists Nowcom as a credit-report reseller to Westlake.

**What it does for buy here pay here** (its BHPH page)
- Portfolio management, with loan changes, extensions and side notes.
- Related finance company (RFC) tracking and portfolio sales through Agora.
- ACH and card processing, with recurring payments.
- A collections queue, plus promise-to-pay tracking.
- Credit bureau reporting through Datalinx, which may require a minimum portfolio size.
- Payment reminders, past-due and insurance-expiration texts.
- Accounting with a QuickBooks link.
- A buyer self-service portal ("My Account") for payments, payoff, history and **insurance uploads**.

**Reach:** 1,485 Capterra reviews with an average of 4.9.

### Where it breaks (what dealers say)
- **The rating is inflated by a review drive.** Many of the top reviews are dated November 27 to 30, 2024 and marked "Vendor Referred – Incentive Offered". Discount the 4.9 accordingly.
- **It gets worse as it grows.** "It's updated regularly and things change to make it 'better' but it's less and less user friendly. It gets more bugs in it and operates slower… [It's] absolutely gone downhill over the last year or so." (Dealer principal, 3/5, switched from Frazer)
- **Old deals are hard to find.** "Finding old deals is a pain to do." This comes up in several reviews.
- **The interface feels crowded and dated.**
  - "Messy UI on the home screen. Needs to be simplified. Dont need to see that much on the main screen."
  - "Lots of clicking to navigate around."
  - "The website looks like it's something made in the early 2000s."
  - "The user interface looks a bit outdated."
- **It keeps logging people out.** "Constantly having to login. It logs you off too soon." Another review is titled "Fix login issues, please".
- **It's large and hard to configure.** "Tough to manage settings since the system is pretty large and as someone who isnt computer savvy that can be challenging."
- **New features ship broken and support is slow.** "Slow support team responses, some functionality is rolled out that isnt properly working yet."
- **It nickel-and-dimes.** "Frazer doesn't nickel and dime you. Pay the $55, and you get the whole system." (DealerRefresh, a 5-year DealerCenter user) Several Capterra cons are about price.
- **It's hard to get data out.** "Very hard to integrate and export data." (DealerRefresh; a second dealer agreed)
- **The mobile app falls short.** Some tasks need a desktop.
- **Texas buy here pay here gap (unverified).** One Reddit comment says it "can't do deferred sales tax with an RFC in Texas and some other states." That's one comment; confirm it before we ever repeat it.

## 3. What this means for a Texas buy here pay here dealer

These are the things neither product does well, and each one is money for Triple J.

1. **Neither tool is built around collections.**
   - Fullpath is ad audiences for franchise stores.
   - DealerCenter's buy here pay here features are a $50 add-on to a lender-owned DMS.
   - Obavia's center is the opposite: money in, money missing, who needs a call. That's already built (bhph-payments.md §1 to §18).
2. **DealerCenter is owned by a lender's sister company.** This is a fact about ownership, not a claim of wrongdoing; we never say more than that in public. But a dealer who carries their own paper may prefer software that has no lender behind it. "No lender owns Obavia" is true and simple.
3. **Texas deferred sales tax is a real compliance risk** (TIADA, "The 60-Day Rule").
   - The rule: buy here pay here dealers may pay sales tax as payments come in.
   - **The trap:** that right is **lost if the title isn't transferred within 60 days of sale**. Then tax is accelerated, with penalties and interest.
   - **The second trap:** it's also lost if the contract is sold or assigned to anyone other than a related finance company.
   - **What auditors check:** they compare the sale date with the transfer date.
   - Nobody markets a watch on this. The Desk already knows the sale date and each payment, so it can carry the per-payment tax ledger and a 60-day title countdown.
4. **Texting at real portfolio volume.**
   - A 150-account weekly portfolio sends one reminder and one receipt per payment: about 150 × 2 × 4.3 ≈ **1,300 texts a month** (our estimate).
   - That is well past DealerCenter's 500 included buy here pay here texts.
5. **Too many screens versus one question per screen.** Every DealerCenter and Cox complaint about the interface (clicks, clutter, dated look, settings, logouts) is the problem the Desk's design already answers.

Deliberately not taken:
- **A customer data platform or ad audiences.** Cox owns that game, and a buy here pay here lot doesn't buy programmatic ads.
- **An AI voice agent on the phones.** Fullpath's is a waitlist and DealerCenter's AI agent is $99 a month. Our edge is the owner seeing the right three things each night, not a bot talking to buyers about their debt. Debt texts also carry legal risk; see the counsel notes in bhph-payments.md.
