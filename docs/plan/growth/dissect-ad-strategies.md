## 3. Systemic logic

### A. Direct-response machine (call funnel)
1. Funnel steps: ad → page (headline, VSL, application with conditional logic) → scheduler → booked call → show → qualified → close.
2. Choose the optimization event. Default: web-pixel standard event fired when a qualified application leads to a booked call. Too little volume: step up to submit-application. Enough volume (50 conversions/week/ad set): step down to qualified-call-held via Conversions API, where the sales team marks the call after it happens and fires the event back to the results column. $2–5M+/month: optimize on close.
3. Always use standard events rather than custom conversions, at any scale, because the channel has far more accumulated data on who performs standard events.
4. Scaling: duplicate the campaign and optimize one step up or one step down (cyclic campaigns). Each event reaches only the subset of TAM probable to perform it; running several events reaches the union. Pick event pairs with the least Venn overlap (schedule vs submit-application overlap heavily; events further apart overlap less). Many cyclic campaigns look like many circles of varying size and overlap.
5. Do not run cyclic campaigns at test budgets: campaigns will show different ROAS and lead intent, and delayed-firing events (purchase, schedule on a webinar funnel) leave the results column blank until the event happens, which confuses the read.
6. Targeting order of control: (1) messaging, (2) the optimization event selected, (3) pixel conditioning, and only after the first two. Pixel holds 180 days and biases to the last two weeks.
7. Diagnostic: wrong people showing up with surface events (lead, complete registration) = messaging problem. Campaign profitable only when optimized on purchase/schedule = over-reliance on pixel; acceptable as a bridge while fixing messaging. Pixel conditioning hits a ceiling in large TAMs; messaging is what lifts it.
8. Remedy when convinced the pixel is poisoned: stop feeding it data, use a new pixel or withhold data, fix messaging.
9. Measurement location: ads-manager results column, fed by web pixel or CAPI.

### B. Venus fly trap 1.0 / 2.0 (content-first, sequence)
1. Target the same cold audience DR ads would target, with video-view objectives.
2. Step 1: one video (1.0) or five videos (2.0) that introduce person/brand/product. Threshold: 10 s of any one video.
3. Step 2: five videos built on the majority hook; threshold 10 s.
4. Step 3: five videos; threshold 10 s.
5. Only then: direct-response ads with calls to action.
6. Rationale for five per step: Andromeda (retrieval) sifts millions to billions of candidate creatives down to a few thousand per user; Lattice (ranking) orders those few thousand per placement, factoring advertiser budgets. More creatives per step give retrieval more context to match.
7. Feedback: cost per call up; show, close, sales feedback up. Use when DR leads are too uneducated to close and reps cannot run education calls.

### C. Forester (content-first, renewable)
1. Bin 1: 10–50 pieces of content, engagement/video-view campaigns, cold audience. Only goal: a 3-second view, which makes the person retargetable.
2. DR ads target "watched 3 s of any bin-1 video, not converted".
3. Bin 2: unlimited content (case studies, testimonials, podcasts, short, long, how-to, product features, demos, organic winners) targeting the same 3-second non-converters, running at the same time as DR ads.
4. Loop: new cold viewers are planted in bin 1 while the retargetable pool is harvested; the pool replenishes. Measured effects: lower CPM to reach, lower cost per DR event, higher show/close, better sales feedback.

### D. Harvester and Tornado (content-first, at scale)
1. Multiple content cycle bins, 50–200 pieces each, multiple stages.
2. Harvester: mass-market audiences who do not identify as prospects (e.g. nurses and Uber drivers converted to high-ticket closers once the in-market and needs-convinced pools were exhausted).
3. Tornado: needs-convinced audiences, when there is an excess of organic content.
4. Selection rule by segment: in-market → plain DR; want cheaper reach → Forester; needs-convinced → Tornado; mass market → Harvester; non-buyer → never.

### E. Hammer them (bolt-on, post-conversion)
1. Bolts on after a conversion and before the money event: booked call → call; webinar opt-in → webinar; low-ticket buy → high-ticket pitch; DM received → set (audience "DM'd in last X days", at least 150 people).
2. Content: 30–50 short form plus 15–50 long form, chosen by the "four quadrant system" (named, not described).
3. Structure: one ad set per piece of content; each ad set excludes people who have watched 3 s of that piece. A person sees each piece once and rotates through the rest.
4. Goal: 15–20 unique pieces per person in the window.
5. Maintenance: a piece with high frequency means viewers are not passing 3 s; pull it.
6. Measured effects: show rate, enthusiasm, condensed DM-to-booked cycle, fewer education calls.
7. Sits inside five "back-end selling systems": confirmation page, value-dense email sequences, setter pre-call practices, shaping AI's answers about you, hammer them.

### F. Overall taxonomy
1. Direct response with pro tips (event selection, cyclic campaigns, messaging-first, CAPI down-funnel optimization).
2. Content-first strategies (Venus fly trap 1.0/2.0, Forester, Harvester, Tornado, ~10 others unnamed).
3. Bolt-on content strategies (Hammer them).
Million-a-month earners use (1) alone or (1) plus (2)/(3).

## 4. Falsifiable claims

| # | Claim | Test |
|---|-------|------|
| 1 | Standard events outperform custom conversions for the same funnel step | Split identical campaigns on a standard event vs an equivalent custom conversion; compare cost per qualified call over the same window |
| 2 | 50 conversions/week/ad set is the floor to exit learning on a down-funnel event | Run ad sets below and above 50/week on a CAPI qualified-call event; observe learning-phase status and CPA stability |

## Ten laws

1. Optimize on standard events, never custom conversions; the channel already knows who performs them.
2. Optimize one step deeper only when you clear 50 conversions per week per ad set.
3. Mark qualified calls through CAPI after the call and let that event train delivery.
4. Each optimization event reaches only its own slice of TAM; stack low-overlap events to widen reach.
5. Messaging and the chosen event control targeting first; pixel conditioning only third, and it fades in 180 days.
6. Wrong people arriving is a messaging failure, not a pixel failure.
7. Direct response has the fewest steps and is always the cheapest default.
8. Content before the ask: three 10-second views, five videos per step, then the call to action.
9. A 3-second view makes a stranger retargetable and cheaper to reach.
10. Between booking and the call, show 15–20 unique pieces once each, never one piece repeatedly.
