## 2. Money logic

Every number and formula as spoken. Ranges verbatim.

### 2.1 Financial model rows (one-call or two-call close)
1. Ad spend
2. Conservative cost per call (choose: cost per scheduled call, or cost per qualified call; speaker optimises on total calls because downstream stats already account for qualified/unqualified)
3. One-call close: show rate → close rate (second-call rows set to 100%)
4. Two-call close: first call show rate → second call booking rate → second call show rate → close rate
5. AOV = cash collected per sale (never revenue; "operate in reality")
6. Gross ROAS
7. Fixed costs against that revenue
8. Team costs against that revenue
9. Sales team commissions
10. Net profit, net ROAS

Rule: "make a conservative projection"; each stat "at the upper end of the worst side of the range".

### 2.2 Cost per call decomposition (traffic side)
- cost per call is determined by three stats: CPM × link click-through rate × click→scheduled call rate
- CPM: $30–$80
- Link click-through rate: ~2% (link CTR, not overall CTR)
- Click → qualified scheduled call: 3–5% ("every click out of a 100 people, you want to convert three to five")
- Expected conservative cost per call: $100–$300 depending on AOV
- $500 or $1,000 cost per call is acceptable with "a much longer sales cycle, a much smaller, more qualified TAM, a substantially larger average order value"
- Cost per call tolerance "is solely determined by the amount you actually cash collect per sale on average"

### 2.3 Funnel stage expectations
- First call show rate (one-call close): ideal 60%+. Seen as low as ~30% ("absolute worst of the worst", "tech issue territory" below 30%), "seen it in the 20s" but rare. Conservative estimate: 40–50%.
- Second call booking rate: ideally 60%+ ("if not higher"); controlled by setters/qualifiers and lead qualification level; a contracted number "might be a lead quality issue or it could be a setter issue".
- Second call show rate: ideally 75%+ (budget section); "at least 70% plus" (sales-team-expectations and bottleneck sections).
- Close rate, one-call: ideal ~25%; usual 20–25%; worst closers 10–15%; 30%+ is above average.
- Close rate, two-call: minimum 35%, "should be much more than that because you added the extra friction".
- AOV: at or above list price when upsells happen on the closing call; often below list in B2C because of "financing cuts like the 15% they'll typically take off the top if not worse" or poor leads.
- AOV governs tolerance: low AOV forces excessively low cost per call, very high show rate, very high close rate; high AOV "you get away with a lot more".
- Speaker's preference: one-call close ("less friction as a marketer"); two-call only with a written justification for the added friction.

### 2.4 Call-volume budget worked example
- 30 days/month, 4 weeks, 4 weekends × 2 days = 8 days off → 22 calling days
- $15,000 spend ÷ $345 cost per call = 43 calls
- 43 ÷ 22 days = 1.95 ≈ 2 calls per closer per day
- Fill three closers: 8 slots/day/closer × 3 closers = 24 calls/day; 24 × 22 days = 528 calls
- 528 × $345 = "$182,60" as transcribed (= $182,160), "about 182 grand"
- At $200 cost per call: $90,000 buys only 450 calls; "about a hundred grand" to fill three closers (528 × $200 = $105,600)
- Budget is set from (a) gambling budget, (b) number of closers to fill, (c) conservative math; "you can prove things out at a smaller level, and you should"
- Example test budget quoted: "$20 grand over 30 days"

### 2.5 Closer math (goal reverse-engineered, one-call close)
Formula chain:
- closes needed = goal ÷ cash collected per sale
- calls taken = closes ÷ close rate
- calls booked = calls taken ÷ show rate
- ad spend = calls booked × cost per call
- calls booked per day = calls booked ÷ 22 calling days
- closers needed = calls per day ÷ 8 slots per closer per day, round up

Worked:
- $1,000,000 ÷ $10,000 = 100 closes
- 100 ÷ 0.20 = 500 calls taken
- 500 ÷ 0.60 = 833 calls booked
- 833 × $150 = $124,950 ad spend
- 833 ÷ 22 = ~38 calls/day
- 38 ÷ 8 = 4.75 → 5 closers
- "less than 0.1% of people according to research ever actually hit" $1M/month

Step-up version: at $300K/month with two closers, target $500K: $500K ÷ cash per sale ÷ close rate ÷ show rate = calls booked; compare with 2 closers × 8 × 22 capacity; if short, hire before the ceiling.

Hiring lead time: best case 1–2 weeks ("you're a killer"); average 4–6 weeks from knowing you need a closer to confident bookings on their calendar; hitting the ceiling means being "stuck for four to six weeks".

### 2.6 Sales-team capacity constants
- 8 calls per closer per day (assumes one-hour calls)
- 22 calling days per month (weekdays)
- Calendar load target: ~80% of booking capacity, never 100%
- Booking window: no bookings more than ~72 hours out
- Weekend calls show and close at lower rates
- 9-to-5 audience: book before/after work or around lunch; B2B audience: mid-day only

### 2.7 Application and scheduler
- Three typed questions is the sweet spot before friction/cost "overextend"
- Separate application and scheduler: ~50% of qualified applicants drop before booking
- Integrated application and scheduler: ~16% average drop-off
- Typeform + Calendly "always got us the best cost per call"
- Alternatives named: iClosed, SalesKit (thousands/month; credit check and income report where legal; AI grading of typed answers; calendar routing), GoHighLevel app + scheduler (separate), HubSpot (separate), ScheduleOnce (separate)
- 40% of Inner Circle poll respondents vibe-coded their own integrated application + scheduler
- Perceived price of generic application/scheduling software: under $100/month, low hundreds at volume

### 2.8 VSSL analytics
- Play rate target: 50%+ (visitors who press play)
- Engagement rate target: 50%+ (retention through the video)
- Zack example: 9% engagement, steep drop in the first 19 seconds → fix the hook only
- Ideal curve: semi-steep initial drop then flat retention to the end
- Thumbnail clip: 5–10 seconds of the video (Wistia or Vidalytics)
- Chaptering: recommended on every VSSL

### 2.9 Back-end selling systems
- Value-dense emails: start 2/day (3 tops); up to 6/day over a 12-hour window ("one almost every other hour") when quality warrants
- Hammer-them remarketing: audience minimum 150 people to serve; ideal window 72 hours (average booking-to-call gap); below 150 bookings in 72 h, widen to a 30-day audience
- Four quadrants of content: questions, second-layer questions, objections, expectations

### 2.10 Named case figures
- Airbnb done-for-you client: 75%+ show rate on cold paid traffic, $22,000 average cash collected per sale, 40%+ close rate. Ad math: ~$1,000,000 property, cost segregation study, 25% down = $250,000 out of pocket plus ~$20,000 fee → ~$400,000 deduction (speaker: "whether true or untrue")
- Jeremy's Inner Circle: $10,000/month; "half circle" $5,000/month; minimum $100,000/month revenue to join; one-year commitment with signed contract; at cap with waitlist; no scheduler on that funnel
- Honesty polls: 70% (IC members) and 80%+ (client's Facebook group) would lie about income

### 2.11 Bottleneck analysis worked example
- CPM $40 (in range), link CTR 2% (in range), page conversion 1.5% (half the floor of 3–5%), show 60%, close 25%, AOV $4,000
- Doubling 1.5% → 3% stays at the bottom of range and doubles bookings, shows, closes and cash: "I cut my cost per call in half and I double my ROAS"
- Rule: pick the single most contracted stat, then "whichever stat is the easiest to double"

### 2.12 Scaling rules
- Classic: +10–30% daily
- Surfing (Tim Burd): +10–30% multiple times a day if cost holds and volume comes through
- Cost per X: extra calls wanted × cost per call = extra spend tomorrow ($100 cost per call × 10 calls = $1,000)
- With cost cap / cost-per-result goal: more aggressive scaling allowed
- Automated bidding: +10–30% every few days, not daily
- "Do the scaling math": 10%/day compounded for 30 days in a sheet; derive calls/day at day 7, 14, 21, 30; derive closers needed by then; subtract hiring lead time to find when to start hiring

## 3. Systemic logic

The machine, in order, with what is measured and what each measurement forces.

1. Pre-launch alignment. Decide whether closers can run education calls. If not, build sales/trust assets (pre-call page: deal list, financials, video breakdowns) so marketing carries education. Measurement: none yet; decision: what to build before spend.
2. Financial model. Fill rows: spend, cost per call, show, (second booking, second show), close, cash per sale, fixed, team, commissions → gross and net ROAS. Decision: whether the bet is worth placing and at what budget.
3. Budget from three inputs: gambling budget (loss you can absorb), closer-fill requirement (slots × closers × 22 days × cost per call), conservative math. Decision: start small, best closer on the line, prove at a smaller level.
4. Closer math. goal → closes → calls taken → calls booked → spend; calls booked ÷ 22 ÷ 8 → closers. Decision: hire before capacity ceiling, offset by 4–6 week hiring lead time.
5. Traffic. Ads with real reasons, unique mechanism, majority hook, process on display. Measurements: CPM ($30–80), link CTR (2%). Decision: fix ads if either is out of range.
6. Page. Headline + VSSL + embedded application (or button). Measurements: play rate (50%+), engagement (50%+), retention curve shape, click→booked (3–5%). Decisions: first-19-seconds drop → rewrite hook; low play rate in a video-averse niche → DSL deck or page sections; low click→booked → lower friction (embed, fewer questions, drop financial qualifier) or raise friction if unqualified volume is the problem.
7. Application. Order: layup multiple choice (may lead-score, may branch) → financial/time qualifier at chosen friction level (none / sophisticated proxy like Fazio's arithmetic / explicit price and income acknowledgement) → third question → remaining multiple choice → name → phone → email. Three typed questions max. Questions must be mutually agreed by sales and marketing; questions only one side wants inflate cost. Measurement: typed-answer length as intent proxy; historical correlation of answers to show/buy (strip questions with no correlation).
8. Scheduler. Integrated with the application in the same step. Measurement: qualified-applicant → booked drop (16% integrated vs 50% separate). Decision: integrate; pixel is conditioned on who books, so wrong bookers compound.
9. Confirmation page (vessel 1). Urgency / due-diligence video first; breakout videos for the things closers are tired of explaining; testimonials and framing. Goal: data to defend the decision to self and others. Measurement: consumption of breakout videos.
10. Value-dense email sequence (vessel 2). 2–3/day rising to 6/day; reminders woven in. Goal: bump decaying interest repeatedly before the call. Sales-ops upgrade: enrichment (People Data Labs, Clay; ZoomInfo for B2B) + brand-voice database → custom sequences per lead.
11. Hammer-them remarketing (vessel 3). Short- and long-form content to the booked-call audience (150 minimum, 72 h ideal, 30 days until scale), covering questions, second-layer questions, objections, expectations. Goal: replicate organic consumption via paid.
12. Setter outreach (vessel 4). Human or AI agent; job is education and value, not just qualification; sends the sales asset. Measurement: connection rate (often highest of all vessels).
13. AI answers (vessel 5). Make information rank where AI models scrape so AI describes the vendor positively (details withheld in the source).
14. Sales ops layer. AI agents: enrich on booking, confirm calls conversationally, match lead to closer strengths, move unresponsive leads from closer to setter calendars, grade typed answers in real time and route calendars.
15. Sales team operation. Measurements: 60% show, 60%+ second booking, 70–75%+ second show, 25% one-call close, 35%+ two-call close, 80% calendar load, 72 h window, time-of-day fit. Roles: educator (raises interest level, can be tagged in live, transitions education → closing call; armed with question sets not decks), cleaner (takes leads once closers can no longer mathematically follow up). Decision: each department accepts accountability for the stats it influences.
16. Feedback loop (sales → ads). Closers report who is showing up; adjust ads and attraction so the right people come through; pixel receives only qualified conversions.
17. Failure branch: bottleneck analysis. Map every stat against its range; find the one or two most contracted; cost-per-call problems point at ads or funnel depending on which of CPM/CTR/click→booked is worse; show or close problems may still point at ads (wrong people). Double the easiest stat. If out of ideas, "you've run out of data to think with" → seek new data.
18. Success branch: scale. 10–30%/day (or surf, cost per X, cost cap); scaling math projects spend and calls at day 7/14/21/30 → closers needed → hire ahead by hiring lead time. Loop: fill calendars → hire closers → fill calendars → hire closers.

## Ten laws

1. Every stat is modelled conservatively; cash collected per sale, never revenue, is the AOV.
2. Cost per call = CPM × link CTR × click-to-booked; tolerable cost per call is set by cash per sale.
3. Goal ÷ cash per sale ÷ close rate ÷ show rate = calls booked; × cost per call = spend.
4. Calls booked ÷ 22 days ÷ 8 slots = closers; hire 4–6 weeks before hitting that ceiling.
5. Friction and intent are the same dial; higher friction buys intent at higher cost and lower volume.
6. One object of attention per page: headline, video, application; everything else is an exit.
7. Integrate application and scheduler: 16% drop instead of 50%.
8. Booking flips the lead from scanner mode to justification mode; feed data through five vessels.
9. Offload education from closers into marketing assets; closers should receive layups or be educators.
10. When it fails, find the single most contracted stat and double the easiest one.
