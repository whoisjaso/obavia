# Dissection: Confirmation page masterclass (Jeremy Haynes)

Source: `docs/plan/transcripts/confirmation-page-masterclass.transcript.txt`. Extraction only. No application to Obavia; that is the Mirror's job.

Scope of the transcript: what to put on the page a person sees immediately after a key action (webinar opt-in, call booking, low-ticket purchase, application). The speaker positions it as the first of his "five back-end selling systems" (confirmation page, value-dense email sequences, setter pre-call practices, "hammer them" retargeting, AI manipulation).

## 3. Systemic logic

The machine, as a numbered sequence.

1. **Lead acts** (opt-in, booking, low-ticket purchase, application). Scanner mode ends; justification mode begins. Education starts here, not earlier.
2. **Page acknowledges the action** in one headline ("You're in"), then puts attention on the **main video**.
3. **Choose the main video type** by one rule:
   - Presenter is the originator of the ideas and the claims must be believed because of who says them → **due-diligence video** (search own name, show best/average/bad outcomes, show dispute rate, lift the skirt).
   - Presenter is a vessel for a grandfather-rule opportunity (real estate, stocks, sales jobs) and the action is capital allocation → **urgency video** built on a third-party, dated, verifiable fact that answers "why now".
   - Never anything other than one of those two.
4. **Choose breakout videos** by only two questions: (a) what are reps tired of answering or struggling to answer on calls; (b) what do you want leads framed on and aware of before the call. Quantity = the count of those items. One to two minutes each, clear question-style titles, stacked individually (side-by-side layout only when consumption is already low). Chapter longer videos.
5. **Host videos where play rate and engagement are measured per video** (Wistia, Vidalytics, etc.). Breaking topics out into separate videos exists *so that* each topic is measured independently.
6. **Testimonials are optional** and come last. Prefer a "worst case" breakout. Never link Trust Pilot. If testimonials are used they must be believable, not cherry-picked.
7. **Decide page density by sales timing and niche**: speed-to-lead call booking → front-load everything; webinar days away → one due-diligence video, push the rest through emails so pitches land on YouTube/webinar where the speaker controls the pitch; blue-collar niche → light page, heavy email and setter; video-native niche → load it up.
8. **Measure** per video: play rate (target 50%+), engagement (target 80%+). Measure email open (40–60%). Measure setter reachability. Measure show rate, close rate, sales-team feedback.
9. **Diagnose with messaging congruency**: say the same things on the page, in the emails and through setters. Then:
   - Lift in show/close/feedback → messaging works.
   - No lift and low play/open/reachability → **consumption problem**; change vehicle (email, setter, retargeting "hammer them", AI), thumbnails, or page weight for that niche.
   - No lift with healthy consumption → **messaging problem**; the topics are selfish or irrelevant; rewrite.
   - Play < 50% with engagement still 80%+ → topic is right, packaging/thumbnail wrong.
   - Engagement < 80% → content is wrong, with certainty.
10. **Route under-consumed key framing** to another of the five systems (emails, setters, retargeting, AI) or repackage it on the page; repeat.
11. **Thumbnails**: many videos → image thumbnails; one or few videos → video thumbnail cut to a moment that states the topic and invites the viewer in.
12. **Post-purchase pages follow the same pattern**: welcome/expectations video, staff introductions by name, UI/navigation videos, support channels, "go activate everything", each as a separate measured video.

Feedback loops: video stats → topic selection; topic selection → show/close rate; show/close rate and rep feedback → messaging congruency verdict; verdict → which of the five systems carries the message next.

## 4. Falsifiable claims

| # | Claim | Test |
|---|-------|------|
| F1 | Correctly chosen breakout videos get 50%+ play rate from page visitors | Instrument each video; compare play rate against 50% over a fixed visitor count |
| F2 | Engagement stays 80%+ when the topic is right, even at 20% play rate | Measure engagement on videos with low play rate; if engagement is also low the topic is wrong, if high the thumbnail is wrong |
| F3 | A single top-of-page video can raise play rates of all videos beneath it from ~20% to 50%+ | A/B the page with and without a main video; compare breakout play rates |
| F4 | Breakouts alone lift show rate ~4%; a fact-based urgency main video lifts it ~19% | Sequential or split test on webinar show rate holding ad source constant |
| F5 | A due-diligence video (searching own name, showing dispute rate) produces a 44% show rate on cold traffic | Replicate with and without the video on a cold-traffic webinar |
| F6 | Testimonial blocks lower trust; a "worst case" video raises it | Split test testimonials vs worst-case breakout; compare show rate and "scam/review" search behaviour |
| F7 | A Trust Pilot link sends visitors to one-star reviews and seeds AI overviews with them | Click-path analytics on the link; search "brand + scam" in AI overviews before and after removal |
| F8 | Email open rates of 40–60% are achievable on confirmation sequences; below that is a consumption problem | Measure opens; test subject/sender/timing changes against the floor |
| F9 | Blue-collar niches do not watch confirmation videos but do read emails and take setter calls | Compare play rate vs email open vs setter connect rate by niche |
| F10 | Short stacked videos are consumed more than one long combined video | Test a single combined video against the same content split into 1–2 minute pieces |
| F11 | Messaging congruency across page, email and setter lets you isolate messaging vs consumption failures | Run identical messaging across three channels; see whether stats move together |
| F12 | A genuine, receipt-backed waitlist video adds revenue to the lesser tier (~$70K/month claimed) | Add/remove the video; measure lesser-tier revenue |
| F13 | Paid-ads sales processes must cash-cycle inside ~30 days to stay profitable | Track days from ad spend to cash per cohort |

## 5. Sales pitch and noise

Ignore for the Mirror:

- Speaker introduction, "cracking million-dollar months", recent $1M/month recurring claim, channel plugs.
- Repeated attacks on "copycats" who teach "one video only" without data.
- Disclaimer block (0.1% probability framing) — legal posture, not mechanism.
- **Jeremy AI** (agentic clone; free confirmation-page scripting tool in the description as "bait"; builds pages; connects Wistia/Vidalytics analytics).
- **Jeremy's Inner Circle** ($10K/month, half circle $5K/month, annual; page audits, weekly calls, SOP vault) — the pricing and waitlist mechanics are kept in sections 1–2 as examples; the invitation itself is pitch.
- **Master Internet Marketing** (seven-week programme, SOPs, example links).
- Promised future videos on email sequences and setter practices.
- Evolutionary-biology story about berries and hunting (illustrative, not evidence).
- "Have fun and go get rich" sign-off and self-mythologising as the "OG" originator.

## Ten laws

1. Before the action the lead scans; after it they justify. Educate only after the action.
2. The main video is either due diligence or urgency; it is never anything else.
3. Originator of the ideas → due diligence. Vessel for a grandfather-rule opportunity → urgency.
4. Urgency must rest on a third-party, dated, verifiable fact; made-up scarcity is discounted.
5. Do the viewer's due diligence for them: show the search, the dispute rate, the bad outcomes.
6. One breakout video per question reps are tired of answering or per frame you need set.
7. Right topics produce 50%+ play and 80%+ engagement; below 80% the content is wrong.
8. One correct top video unlocked every video beneath it: 20% → 50%+ play, +19% show.
9. Say the same thing on page, email and setter; then diagnose consumption versus messaging.
10. Testimonials are the least believed asset; answer "how bad could it get" instead.
