# 04. The regulator and counsel

Angle: a TxDMV Enforcement investigator, a DTPA plaintiff's lawyer, a Comptroller auditor, the FTC rule and Apple App Review, each walking one Obavia sale. All people are fictional. Statutes were checked today where possible; the rest is flagged at the end.

**The headline before the scenarios.** The blueprint's §4 says "advertising for others in your own name is not brokering" and cites Dealer Manual §4.34. That is the *new-vehicle* broker rule (Occ. Code §2301.002(3), 43 TAC §215.84). The used-vehicle rule is 43 TAC §215.85, verified today from Cornell LII, and it is worse than the blueprint thinks:

> "(a) ... 'directly or indirectly' includes the practice of arranging or offering to arrange a transaction involving the sale of a used motor vehicle for a fee, commission, or other valuable consideration. ... (b) A buyer referral service, program, plan, club, or any other entity that accepts a fee for arranging a transaction involving the sale of a used motor vehicle is required to meet the requirements for and obtain a GDN, unless ... (3) All participants pay the same fee ... a weekly, monthly, or annual fee ... (4) A person is not to be charged a fee on a per referral fee basis or any other basis that could be considered a transaction-related fee. (5) The program does not set or suggest to the dealer any price of a motor vehicle or a trade-in. (6) The program does not advertise or promote its plan in a manner that implies that the buyer, as a customer of that program, receives a special discounted price that cannot be obtained unless the customer is referred through that program."

Under Structure A, without a GDN, that rule kills a success fee, a buyer's premium and a per-listing fee (a transaction-related fee on its face). It also reaches the "wholesale band drawn beside the field" in Let It Go ((b)(5), a suggested price) and the "Get the dealer price" marketing ((b)(6)). The §1802 auctioneer exemptions answer a different question (auctioneer licence); they say nothing about §503.021 indirect dealing.

## Scenario 1. TxDMV Enforcement, complaint file on one sale (the "happy path" sale, audited)

Buyer Marisol Treviño, Harlingen, bought a 2015 Nissan Altima from Caldera Auto Sales (fictional, Hidalgo County, independent GDN) through Obavia at a $6,400 hammer. Three weeks later she filed a complaint on the TxDMV site: "no title yet, dealer says the app has it."

Investigator Dwayne Pickett sends Caldera a records request (43 TAC §215.144) for the deal jacket (130-U, Buyers Guide with the buyer's acknowledgment, bill of sale, odometer disclosure, title copy), proof of the 30-day title filing (§501.0234; the Desk's 45-day clock is the seller-financed *tax* deadline, not this one), the ad as it ran, and the money trail: who took the deposit, who took the hammer, when Caldera got paid.

What he sees. The 130-U seller line reads "Caldera Auto Sales LLC (Roberto Caldera)", signed by Roberto, which matches webDEALER's rule and his licence. Good. Sales price box: $6,400. The Obavia receipt shows Marisol paid $6,400 plus a $320 "buyer's premium" to Obavia, LLC. He asks why $320 of consideration tied to the vehicle is not in the sales price box, and who Obavia is. Obavia has no GDN. He pulls the app listing: "Dealer floor" and "Retail" bars, "Get the dealer price", no dealer name on the screen. He opens a second file: Obavia, §503.021 indirect dealing under §215.85, plus an advertising violation (43 TAC Subchapter F, ad does not identify the dealer). Caldera gets a notice for allowing an unlicensed person to arrange sales.

At 9pm Roberto texts the Obavia owner: "I got a letter from DMV. It says YOUR app. What do I tell them. I'm not losing my licence for a Altima."

Change: every lot screen carries the dealer's legal name and GDN number (small, but on the screen and in every Reach post); the 130-U sales price equals every dollar the buyer pays for the car, and nothing is collected by a non-GDN entity per transaction under Structure A.

## Scenario 2. The transmission fails in three days (DTPA plaintiff's lawyer)

Buyer Kendrick Oyelaran, Fort Worth, wins a 2013 Chevy Traverse, hammer $5,100, from Lonestar Budget Motors (fictional). Day three the transmission slips, day four it's dead. Repair quote $3,900. He finds attorney Priya Natarajan, who takes DTPA cases on contingency because §17.50 gives fees and up to treble damages for knowing conduct.

She reads what Kendrick signed: an FTC Buyers Guide with AS IS checked, a bill of sale saying "as-is, no warranty". Bus. & Com. Code §2.316(c) lets "as is" exclude implied warranties, and *Singleton v. LaCoure* (verified today) holds a compliant disclaimer leaves no warranty for the DTPA to borrow. So she goes to what survives an as-is: (1) misrepresentation, §17.46(b)(5),(7),(24): the six-tap condition pass showed "starts/drives" green, plus the walkaround and undercarriage video, and "the grade comes from the photos". A representation of fact. She subpoenas the Desk: the car sat 94 days and a service note says "trans slipping, send to auction." Knowing non-disclosure; as-is does not cover it (*Prudential v. Jefferson Assocs.* fraudulent-inducement carve-out). (2) §17.42: the DTPA cannot be waived except by a signed, conspicuous, counsel-represented waiver; any "you waive DTPA" line in Obavia terms is void and looks bad. (3) Defendants: Lonestar, and Obavia for the grade it designed and published with a verified look. DTPA needs no privity; *Amstadt* (verified) only shields an upstream party whose representation never reached the consumer, and this one did, in our app.

Demand letter (§17.505, 60 days): $3,900 repair, $1,200 rental, fees, trebled if knowing. Lonestar's owner texts at 9pm: "Your app made me click 'drives'. It drove. Who's paying this lawyer?"

Change: the condition pass must be the dealer's statement, in the dealer's name, with the exact words shown to the buyer ("the dealer says: starts and drives on [date]"), stored with timestamp; the Desk's service notes for a car must be surfaced to the dealer at Let It Go with a one-line "anything a buyer should know?" and a forced answer; Obavia terms contain no DTPA waiver, and Obavia describes itself as the publisher of the dealer's statements, nothing more.

## Scenario 3. The investigator runs the broker question across each fee design

Pickett's second file. He tests four fee shapes against §215.85, and I give the answer as he would write it.

- **Dealer subscription (flat monthly, same for every dealer, no per-car charge).** Fits (b)(3). Survives if (b)(1)–(7) also hold: no exclusive territories, no price suggestion, no "special price only here" advertising, and compliance with Subchapter F advertising. The product today fails (b)(5) (wholesale band beside the floor; "Retail" bar) and (b)(6) ("Get the dealer price"). Those can be fixed by words, and must be.
- **Per-listing fee.** "Any other basis that could be considered a transaction-related fee." Fails on its face unless Obavia holds a GDN. Peak avoids this because its Texas operation runs under a licensed auctioneer and (apparently) a GDN; we are not Peak.
- **Success fee to the dealer.** A commission for arranging a used-vehicle sale. The clearest violation. Fails.
- **Buyer's premium to Obavia.** The same, from the other side, plus a sales-tax problem (Scenario 5) and a "who is the seller" problem on the 130-U. Fails under A. Under B it is Obavia's own sale and ordinary.

Exposure: civil penalties per violation per day for unlicensed activity (§503.094 ff., Occ. Code §2301.801; amounts not verified today), and the dealer partner's licence as collateral. The dealer bears the risk of our fee design.

## Scenario 4. Is the online auction the dealer's "location"? (and the edge case: the car isn't on the lot)

§503.021 (verified today): a GDN is needed "for each location from which the person conducts business as a dealer." §503.027(a) (verified, current text): "A dealer must hold a general distinguishing number for a consignment location unless the consignment location is a wholesale motor vehicle auction." §503.024(d) (verified): an auctioneer is not dealing when title does not pass to them and, for a GDN holder's vehicles, "the auction is conducted at the location for which the general distinguishing number was issued."

Under A the dealer consigns nothing: the car sits on its licensed lot, the dealer is seller of record, the site is advertising, signing and pickup happen at the lot. That reads as a sale from the licensed location, like an eBay Motors sale. The edge case breaks it: dealer Yolanda Marsh (fictional) lists a car parked at her cousin's body shop and sends the buyer there. Now the sale is conducted from an unlicensed location (curbstoning) and Obavia facilitated it. Under B, cars consigned to Obavia but sitting on dealers' lots are the open question: each lot is licensed, but Obavia's own premises must meet §503.032, and the department may treat every lot as a place Obavia does business from.

Change: Let It Go requires the pickup address to equal a licensed location on the dealer's licence record (we already pull the TxDMV list); any other address is refused, not warned.

## Scenario 5. Comptroller audit, the buyer's premium, and the county rejects the title

Tax Code §152.002(a) (verified): total consideration is the amount paid for the vehicle "without deducting ... the cost of material, labor or service, interest paid, loss, or any other expense." Subsection (b)'s exclusions (cash discounts, repair labor, finance charges, trade-ins, post-sale transport, VIT) do not mention an auction premium; Dealer Manual 7.1 excludes only doc, inspection, finance, title and registration fees. Peak taxes price plus premium. If Obavia collects a premium and Caldera's 130-U shows only the hammer, the auditor assesses Caldera 6.25% on the premium plus 5%/10% penalty, and Caldera's bookkeeper texts at 9pm. If the premium is on the 130-U, Caldera is reporting money it never received: the §215.85 problem again.

County: clerk Bettina Ruiz in Cameron County rejects the package because the 130-U price ($6,400) does not match the bill of sale ($6,720 with premium). The fix is one number on every document.

## Scenario 6. The Used Car Rule at pickup, the deposit, the out-of-state buyer, and the second sale (repeat use)

**FTC.** 16 CFR 455.2(a)(1) (verified): the Guide is "displayed prominently and conspicuously in any location on a vehicle," both sides readable, "before you offer a used vehicle for sale." An in-app PDF is not on the vehicle, and at public auctions "the auction company must comply" (FTC Dealer's Guide). Under A the dealer hangs the Guide when Let It Go is tapped; the Desk's "Is The Buyers Guide On The Window?" becomes a required photo of it on the glass. §455.3's copy to the buyer is the signed one. Under B Obavia is jointly responsible and verifies that photo.

**Deposit.** Two paths. A Stripe Connect charge with the dealer as the connected account and Obavia as platform: the money is the dealer's at authorisation, Obavia never holds it, and Finance Code §152.004(2) (verified; ch. 151 was recodified as ch. 152) exempts an agent of the payee under a written agreement where the payor's obligation is extinguished on receipt. A hold or charge into Obavia's own balance for later release is Obavia holding other people's money against a future event: that is escrow-shaped and at minimum invites a money-transmission question, and it is also a consideration flow tied to the transaction under §215.85. Under A: Connect, dealer as payee, Obavia as agent of payee, full stop. Under B: Obavia's own deposit, like Peak.

**Out-of-state.** Buyer Tomas Villanueva drives from Lawton, Oklahoma, wins, and wants to register in Oklahoma. Texas 6.25% is not due on a vehicle removed from the state by a non-resident (Tax Code §152.092, not verified today) but the dealer must document it; webDEALER can't title it in Oklahoma; the buyer needs the assigned title in hand and Oklahoma's own forms. The app's "Plates" tile is wrong for him and the hook "plates mailed to you" is false. Change: at ID scan, a non-Texas licence switches the third tile to "Title handed to you at pickup" and shows the Texas tax position only after the dealer confirms the out-of-state affidavit.

**Bond.** §503.033 (verified): $50,000, conditioned on "the transfer by the applicant of good title to each motor vehicle the applicant offers for sale." Manual 3.12: the claimant needs a judgment first, then claims on the surety; months. Obavia's title-watch and a payout held until the title step goes green are the real protection.

**Repeat use.** Marisol bids again two months later. Pickett counts how many Caldera cars went through Obavia and asks whether "Sell it back here" routed her old car to a partner dealer for a fee: a per-referral fee, (b)(4). Change: it pays Obavia nothing per referral.

**Apple.** Guideline 3.1.3(e) "Goods and Services Outside the App" (heading verified in the November 2025 PDF; body only seen reproduced): physical goods consumed outside the app must not use IAP; Apple Pay or card is fine. A car qualifies. Nothing found on auctions as such; the live question in review is who the merchant of record is, which must match the structure.

## The four questions, from this angle

1. **A to launch, B as target, or straight to B?** A is only safe in its boring form: flat subscription, equal for all dealers, no per-car money to Obavia, no price suggestions, no "dealer price" advertising, dealer as merchant of record on Connect. Launchable with one dealer in weeks. B costs a GDN, a $50,000 bond, §503.032 premises, VIT filings, and puts Obavia on the 130-U; six months minimum, and the only shape in which the premium, a deposit in our balance and the price band are all lawful. Launch A-boring, file for B on day one, never launch "A with a success fee" for a single day.
2. **Premium or dealer-side fee?** Under A, neither; subscription only. Under B a single-digit premium is lawful but is taxable consideration (a 5% premium costs the buyer 5.3%); a seller-side fee is cleaner for tax.
3. **As-is only, or a 48-hour starts-and-drives?** As-is only, and even then the condition pass is a representation as-is does not cover. "Starts and drives" is an express warranty: it goes in the Buyers Guide warranty box, brings Magnuson-Moss, and under A is the dealer's warranty, not ours. Launch as-is with arbitration limited to title, odometer and VIN; add the guarantee only per car when the dealer chooses it and the Guide prints it.
4. **Deposit; refundable on a loss?** A $250 authorisation hold via Connect on the dealer's account, released on loss, charged only when a winner fails to sign within 48 hours, as the dealer's disclosed liquidated damages. A non-refundable deposit on a loss is a transaction fee to the platform (§215.85) and a §17.46 unconscionability claim.

## Three changes I would fight for

1. **Rewrite blueprint §4 around 43 TAC §215.85, not Dealer Manual §4.34.** Every fee, price bar and ad line is tested against (b)(1)–(7); the wholesale band beside the floor and "Get the dealer price" leave the Structure A build.
2. **Dealer name and GDN on every screen and post; one sales price on every document; Buyers Guide photographed on the glass before the auction opens; pickup address locked to a licensed location.** Four mechanical rules that close four enforcement files.
3. **The condition pass is the dealer's signed statement, with the Desk's service history shown at Let It Go and a forced "anything a buyer should know?" line, timestamped.** It is what makes as-is hold on day three and what we will want when subpoenaed.

## Three facts I am not sure of

1. **Whether the §215.85(b) safe harbour reaches a live bidding platform at all,** or is for referral clubs only, so that running an auction needs a GDN whatever the fee shape. No opinion or case found; Peak's Texas setup suggests the department expects a licence behind an online public auction.
2. **Sales tax on a buyer's premium.** §152.002 includes "any other expense" and excludes no premium, and Peak taxes it, but no Comptroller ruling on auction premiums was found.
3. **Apple's current 3.1.3(e) wording and review practice on vehicle auctions**; only the heading verified. Also from memory: Tax Code §152.092, §501.0234's 30-day clock, and the penalty amounts.

## Counsel questions, in priority order

1. Does 43 TAC §215.85 apply to Obavia under A, and does a flat monthly subscription with no per-transaction charge, no price suggestion and compliant advertising fall inside §215.85(b)? In writing; consider a TxDMV informal opinion request.
2. If not: can Obavia run the auction without a GDN at all under §503.021 and §503.024(d)? Independent GDN, Wholesale Auction GDN, or both, and what premises satisfies §503.032 for an online operation?
3. Under B, is the premium total consideration under §152.002, who reports it on the 130-U, and does Obavia become seller of record (title passing through it, §503.024(d)(1) lost)?
4. Deposit custody: does Finance Code §152.004(2) cover a Connect hold with the dealer as payee; is a forfeitable deposit enforceable liquidated damages rather than an unconscionable practice?
5. Does Obavia's design of the six-tap condition pass make it a speaker under §17.46(b), and what wording and records keep it the dealer's statement?
6. Used Car Rule online: who is the auction company under A and B, what display satisfies §455.2 when the consumer never sees the car before bidding, and does a Spanish interface require the Spanish Guide (§455.5)?
7. Advertising under 43 TAC Subchapter F: dealer identification on lot screens and Reach posts, floor/retail bars as price representations, "No dealer licence" as a claim.
8. Out-of-state buyers: tax documentation, title hand-over, geofencing "plates mailed to you."
9. Express-warranty mechanics for a later "starts and drives" option: Guide box, Magnuson-Moss, warrantor.
10. Apple: merchant of record in review for A and B; the 3.1.3(e) reading for a deposit hold.
