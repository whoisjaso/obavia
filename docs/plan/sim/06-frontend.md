## 2. Lego adjacencies, decision by decision

| Decision | Piece A | Piece B | What the user infers | Where it misleads (and the cost) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Is this a deal? | Floor bar | Retail bar | "I'm somewhere under retail." | The buyer reads the floor as the likely price. In a seven-day auction with soft close the hammer lands well above the floor; a buyer anchored at $4,200 feels cheated at $5,150. **Fix:** the floor bar is only shown until the first bid; after that the bars become "Bid" and "Retail". |
| How much will I really pay? | Hammer price | Retail | "Hammer is what I pay." | Tax 6.25 %, the doc fee, state fees and any premium are not in the hammer. On a $5,150 hammer that is about $650 more. **Fix:** on Bid sheet, the total sits directly under the hammer, same font, smaller: $5,150 / $5,802 total. Two numbers, no sentence. The second number is not optional; it is the one that protects us under DTPA. |
| Should I bid now? | Ring (time) | Price | "Little ring left, go now." | Soft close means the ring lies in the last two minutes: it refills. A buyer who learnt the ring means "ending" will feel tricked by the extension. **Fix:** in the last two minutes the ring changes colour (blue) and animates a refill per bid; one blue pulse teaches it. |
| Deposit vs bid | Deposit chip | Bid button | "The $250 is part of my bid." | It isn't; it's a hold. **Fix:** the chip sits beside the button with a hollow (not filled) treatment; a filled chip means money moved. The visual grammar: hollow = held, filled = paid. |
| Max | Max wheel | Price | "Max is what I'll pay." | A proxy bid can land below max. **Fix:** label the wheel with "up to $X" as its value; after setting, the price shows the current bid, not the max, and the chip "up to $5,600" sits beside it hollow. |
| What next after winning | ID · Sign · Plates tiles | The total above | "Do these three, get plates." | The tiles promise plates to anyone; a buyer with an out-of-state licence or a lien complication won't get plates from the dealer's filing. **Fix:** the ID tile is first on purpose; the Plates tile stays grey (not just unfilled) until ID passes the field rules. |
| Dealer: where to set the floor | Money field | Cost basis mark | "Floor should be above my cost." | That is sunk-cost anchoring, the thing that keeps losers on lots. Pairing floor with cost basis invites the dealer to set the floor at cost and get no bids. **Fix:** cost basis is not shown; the wholesale band and the days-on-lot ring are the two pieces. The dealer infers "the lane would give me the band; a floor inside it beats the lane after fees." |
| Dealer: how long | 3 · 7 · 10 | The days-on-lot number | "This car has been here 61 days; 3 more is nothing." | None; this is a benign inference. |
| Dealer: live, no bids | Clean ring | Bold floor line | "The floor is the problem." | Could be the photos. **Fix:** Watching count beside the floor: many watchers and no bids means price; no watchers means reach. Three pieces, still no sentence. |

## 5. The five hardest UI problems

1. **Hammer is not the price.** The buyer's eye anchors on the big number; the law, the dealer and the tax office all deal in the total. Solution: two numbers, stacked, same typeface, 2:1 size, on every screen where money moves (Lane after first bid, Bid, Won, Me). No sentence. The total is computed by the Desk's existing `money.ts` so it is the same number the bill of sale prints.
2. **The soft close looks like cheating.** A ring that refills reads as manipulation. Solution: a different colour and a different motion for the last two minutes, identical every time, and a one-time animated overlay the first time a user sees a refill. Push copy carries "2:00 left". Never a variable extension.
3. **Silence on outbid vs silence on failure.** The doctrine's asymmetry works for outbid and breaks for a rejected bid at close, a declined deposit, or a county kickback. Solution: the only sentences in the app are failure stamps with a server time ("Closed 0.4 s before your bid"), and they live on the object (the price, the tile), never in a modal.
4. **Drag-to-bid is inaccessible and dangerous in a pocket.** Solution: the drag only arms; the bid requires the sheet's button; VoiceOver gets the adjustable trait plus double-tap-and-hold; Android gets the same two-step; a bid over 2× the current price asks for a second heavy press (hold 600 ms) with no text, the button fills as you hold.
5. **The legally required reads inside a wordless app.** The Buyers Guide, the bill of sale and the 130-U must be read and signed. Solution: keep the Desk's scroll-to-end gate, highlight the buyer's own fields in the PDF so the eye has a path, reuse one signature with logged consent, and put the physical Buyers Guide at pickup on the dealer's checklist (the Rule says "on the vehicle"). Don't pretend the read away; make it the one deliberate slow moment in the flow, like the Apple Pay sheet is.

## 6. The four brief questions, UX only

1. **A to launch, B as target.** From the glass, A changes one thing: the dealer's name and city must be on Car and on Sold, and the buyer pays the dealer. That is two words and one Apple Pay payee. B hides the dealer until the win, which is cleaner but means the buyer meets a stranger at pickup; UX prefers A's honesty at launch.
2. **Zero buyer's premium, dealer-side fee.** Every fee on the buyer's side adds a number the buyer must read next to the hammer. One extra line (tax) is tolerable; two (tax + premium) turns the Bid sheet into a receipt. Single-digit premium also breaks the "hammer vs retail" adjacency subtly.
3. **As-is only at launch.** A 48-hour guarantee adds a conditional on the Buyers Guide and a word on the lane ("Guaranteed") that invites the false inference "everything is fine". The six-dot grade already carries "starts/drives". Add the guarantee later as a filled dot, not a word.
4. **$250 hold, refundable on a loss, forfeited on a no-pay.** A hold is one Apple Pay sheet with no second screen. $50 is too small to change behaviour and $500 kills first bids from bus-riders. The chip stays hollow while held, filled when applied, and vanishes on a loss with no sound.

## 7. Three changes I'd fight for

1. **Show the total under the hammer everywhere.** The blueprint's "price, big" alone is the lawsuit and the 9 pm text.
2. **Drop cost basis from the Floor screen.** The blueprint pairs the money field with cost basis and the wholesale band; cost basis anchors the dealer at a floor nobody bids on. Days-on-lot ring and wholesale band only.
3. **Make failure stamps the only sentences.** Outbid stays silent; rejected-at-close, declined hold and county kickback get one server-timed line on the object.

## 8. Three facts I'm not sure of

1. Whether E-SIGN/UETA plus the Desk's ceremony covers a **typed-name signature fallback for VoiceOver users** on the 130-U, or whether TxDMV expects a drawn or wet signature for that form.
2. Whether the FTC Used Car Rule's "displayed on the vehicle" is satisfied by in-app display before the bid plus a physical Guide at pickup, or whether the physical Guide must exist **during** the auction at the lot.
3. Whether iOS lets an App Store app present **Apple Pay for a hold** (authorise-only) with the "held, not charged" wording inside the sheet summary, or only as a line above it; Stripe's manual-capture path is what I assumed.
