§Recitals & Parties

This Weekly Motor Vehicle Rental Agreement (this “Agreement”) is made and entered into as of the Effective Date by and between OBAVIA, a Texas limited liability company with its principal place of business at 8774 Almeda Genoa Road, Houston, Texas 77075 (the “Company,” “we,” “us,” or “our”), and the individual identified and signing on the Signature & Initials Page (the “Renter,” “you,” or “your”). The Company and the Renter are each a “Party” and together the “Parties.”

A. The Company’s business. The Company is engaged in the trade and business of renting motor vehicles to the public. The Company owns, or holds the right to rent, the vehicle described in Section 2 (the “Vehicle”) and rents it to the Renter for the Renter’s own use. The Renter — not the Company — directs, controls, and is responsible for the day-to-day operation of the Vehicle during the Rental Period. The Company does not direct how, when, or where the Renter drives.

What this means
We rent cars for a living, and once the keys are in your hand, you’re the one driving. Because we’re a rental company and you control the driving, federal law (49 U.S.C. § 30106) means we, as the owner, are not automatically on the hook for how the car gets driven. That protection only holds if this stays a real rental — which it is.

B. This is a true rental. This Agreement is a true rental of a motor vehicle and nothing more. The Renter acquires no ownership interest, no equity, and no option to purchase the Vehicle. No portion of any rent or other amount the Renter pays applies toward ownership or a purchase price. This Agreement is not a lease-purchase, a rent-to-own arrangement, a retail installment sale, or any form of financing. Title to the Vehicle remains with the Company (or its owner) at all times.

What this means
You’re renting, not buying. No matter how many weeks you pay, you never start to “own” the car and none of your rent builds toward owning it. If you ever want a car of your own, that’s a separate conversation — this paper isn’t a path to it.

  • Device(s) — the GPS/telematics unit and the starter-interrupt (payment-assurance) unit installed in the Vehicle, as disclosed in Sections 13 and 14.
  • Default — any of the events listed in Section 15.
  • Condition Record — the dated, photo-documented record of the Vehicle’s condition taken at handoff, described in Section 2.

3Term — Open-Ended Weekly

3.1  Continuous weekly rental. This is a continuous, open-ended rental billed by the Rental Week. It has no fixed end date. Each Rental Week automatically renews into the next unless and until either Party ends the rental under Section 3.2. There is no minimum number of weeks, and the rental does not convert into ownership, a longer-term lease, or a purchase by the passage of time.

3.2  Ending the rental. Either Party may end this rental at any time, for any reason or no reason, by giving the other Party at least END NOTICE HOURS of notice. On the end date, the Renter returns the Vehicle to the Company’s address with all keys, equipment, and documents. Rent is owed through the end of the rental as provided in Section 4. Ending the rental does not erase amounts already owed, and Section 15 still applies if the rental ends because of a Default.

What this means
You’re not locked into a contract length. You can walk away with a little notice, and so can we. Keep paying and the car stays yours to drive, week after week — but it never becomes your car.

4Rent & Billing

4.1  Weekly rate. The standard Rent is $350.00 per Rental Week, subject only to the Hardship Bridge in Section 6 during the weeks it applies.

4.2  Billing cycle and due day. Rent is billed in advance for each Rental Week and is due on the same day of each week as the Effective Date (the “Due Day”). The first Rental Week is due at handoff.

4.3  Sole accepted method — card on file. Payment is by automatic charge to the valid, non-prepaid debit or credit card the Renter keeps on file (see Section 5). This is the only accepted method. The Company does not accept cash, checks, money orders, peer-to-peer transfers, or prepaid or gift cards. Debit cards are fully accepted; only prepaid cards are excluded.

4.4  Application of payments. Payments are applied first to past-due Rent, then to current Rent, then to any authorized fees and pass-through amounts. A receipt is available for every payment on request.

5Payment Authorization & Surcharge

5.1  Card on file & auto-charge authorization. The Renter keeps a valid, non-prepaid debit or credit card on file and authorizes the Company to automatically charge that card, without further action by the Renter (an “off-session” charge), for Rent and other authorized amounts as they come due each Rental Week. The Renter must maintain a working card on file for the whole Rental Period; if a card is declined or removed, the Renter promptly provides a replacement non-prepaid card. This authorization continues until the rental ends and all amounts are paid.

5.2  Card surcharge. A credit-card surcharge may apply to credit cards only. Debit cards are never surcharged. Any surcharge will not exceed the Company’s actual cost of accepting the card.

What this means
You keep a working debit or credit card on file and we run your weekly payment automatically, so there’s nothing to remember each week. A regular bank debit card is perfectly fine — only prepaid or gift cards aren’t accepted. If you use a credit card, there may be a small surcharge that only covers what the card company charges us — never more, and never on a debit card.

6Hardship Bridge Program

6.1  The bridge. The Hardship Bridge gives every new Renter a softer start on the same terms. For Rental Weeks 1 and 2 only, Rent is reduced by 20%, subject to a non-waivable minimum of $100.00 per week. Beginning in Rental Week 3, Rent automatically reverts to the full standard rate of $350.00 per week.

6.2  Equal terms. The Hardship Bridge is offered to everyone on identical terms. It is not a discretionary favor, is not negotiated case by case, and does not depend on anyone’s financial situation. It is simply built into the first two weeks.

What this means
Your first two weeks are 20% off (but never below $100), to help you get rolling. From week three on, it’s the regular $350. Everyone gets the same deal — nothing special asked of you, nothing held over you.

7Late Payment

7.1  Grace period. If Rent is not paid on the Due Day, the Renter has a grace period of three (3) calendar days before any late fee attaches.

7.2  Late fee. If Rent remains unpaid after the grace period, a single late fee of $75.00 applies for that occurrence. This is the only late fee, and it is the same $75.00 wherever a late fee appears in this Agreement. It is a reasonable estimate of the Company’s administrative cost of handling a late payment, not a penalty.

7.3  Consequence ladder. The Company handles late Rent in a calm, consistent order: (1) a friendly reminder; (2) the single $75.00 after the grace period; (3) an attempt to charge the payment method on file under Section 5; and then, only if the balance is still unpaid, (4) the enforcement steps in Section 15, each with the notice that Section requires.

What this means
If a payment is late, you get a short grace window first. After that there’s one flat fee — the same amount every time, just covering our paperwork, not a punishment. We start with a reminder, not a threat, and nothing escalates without notice.

8Security Deposit

8.1  Amount. The security deposit is SECURITY DEPOSIT, collected at handoff.

8.2  What it covers. The deposit secures the Renter’s obligations under this Agreement. It may be applied only to actual, itemized amounts the Renter owes — unpaid Rent, unpaid authorized fees, pass-through amounts under Section 12, or chargeable damage beyond normal wear under Section 18. It is not additional rent and is not the Company’s to keep absent an actual amount owed.

8.3  Return. Within fourteen (14) days after the Vehicle is returned and the rental ends, the Company will return the deposit, less any amounts actually owed, together with a written, line-item statement of any deductions. If nothing is owed, the full deposit is returned.

What this means
Your deposit is your money, held in case something is owed at the end. When you return the car, you get it back within two weeks — in full if you don’t owe anything, or with a clear itemized list if anything is deducted. No vague “fees,” no guessing.

9Insurance — Renter-Carried

9.1  Renter carries the coverage. The Renter maintains the Renter’s own full-coverage motor vehicle insurance — including liability, collision, and comprehensive coverage — continuously for the entire Rental Period. The insurance is the Renter’s, the risk is the Renter’s, and the coverage must remain in force every day the Renter has the Vehicle.

9.2  Proof and notice. The Renter provides proof of coverage at handoff and again whenever the Company reasonably requests it, and names or notifies the Company as the policy requires (for example, as an additional interest or loss payee). The Renter tells the Company promptly of any change, lapse, or cancellation.

9.3  Lapse is a Default. If the Renter’s coverage lapses, is canceled, or falls below full coverage, that is an immediate Default. The Company may then place its own coverage on the Vehicle (at the Renter’s cost) and/or recover the Vehicle under Sections 15 and 16.

What this means
The insurance on this car is yours and has to stay active the whole time you drive it. Keep it current and show proof when we ask. If it lapses, that’s a serious problem — you can’t legally drive, and we may need the car back — so don’t let it slip.

10Authorized Use & Drivers

10.1  Lawful use, authorized drivers. The Vehicle is used lawfully and only by the Renter and any Authorized Driver. The Renter does not sublet, re-rent, loan, or assign the Vehicle to anyone else, and does not let an unlicensed or uninsured person drive it.

Express authorization — delivery & rideshare

10.2  Commercial delivery and rideshare use is expressly authorized. The Renter may use the Vehicle for commercial delivery and rideshare work — including food and package delivery and ride-hail platforms — provided the Renter maintains insurance appropriate to that commercial use under Section 9. This use is permitted, not prohibited.

What this means
Driving for delivery or rideshare is fine here — that’s expected. Just make sure your insurance actually covers that kind of work. The one rule: only you and approved drivers use the car, and you don’t rent it out to anyone else.

11Maintenance & Mechanical Responsibility

11.1  Company handles major mechanical. The Company is responsible for major mechanical repair arising from normal use, performed through its own shop or a shop it designates. The Renter should bring the Vehicle in promptly when asked so the Company can keep it roadworthy.

11.2  Renter handles routine care. The Renter handles day-to-day care: checking and topping fluids, keeping tires at proper pressure, keeping the Vehicle reasonably clean, and reporting any warning light, noise, leak, or problem promptly.

11.3  Renter-caused damage. Damage caused by neglect, misuse, abuse, or by continuing to drive after a known fault or warning is the Renter’s responsibility, including the resulting repair cost.

11.4  Mileage. Mileage is unlimited, unless a specific mileage limit is stated in writing.

What this means
Big repairs from normal driving are on us, through our shop. The everyday stuff — fluids, tire pressure, keeping it clean, and telling us when something’s wrong — is on you. If a small problem gets ignored and turns into a big one, that part’s on you.

12Tolls, Citations & Tickets — Pass-Through

12.1  Renter’s responsibility. Tolls, parking tickets, traffic citations, red-light and speed-camera charges, and similar amounts incurred while the Renter has the Vehicle are the Renter’s responsibility.

12.2  Pass-through plus admin fee. When the Company receives and processes one of these items, it passes the actual charge through to the Renter, plus a flat administrative fee of $30.00 per item processed. The Company provides the underlying documentation for each item on request.

What this means
Tolls and tickets you run up are yours to pay. If one comes to us first, we forward it to you at cost, plus one small flat fee for the time it takes to handle it — and we’ll show you the original charge.

13GPS / Telematics Disclosure & Consent

13.1  Disclosure. The Vehicle contains a GPS/telematics device. The Company uses it for vehicle location, recovery, theft response, and routine account management. The Renter consents to its presence and use for these purposes.

What this means
The car can report its location. We use that to find it if it’s stolen, to recover it if we have to, and to keep accounts straight — not to watch your day. We’re telling you it’s there so there are no surprises.
Renter initials — GPS / telematics consent

14Starter-Interrupt / Payment-Assurance Device — Texas Disclosure & Consent

14.1  Disclosure. The Vehicle is equipped with a starter-interrupt (payment-assurance) device. The Renter is entitled to a clear, plain explanation of how it works, and the Company gives it here:

  • The device does not and cannot disable the Vehicle while it is moving. It has no effect on a Vehicle in motion.
  • The device can only prevent a future start — it stops the engine from starting next time, and never interferes with steering, braking, or a running engine.
  • The Company gives advance notice before any disable tied to nonpayment, so the Renter has a chance to pay or make arrangements first.
  • Reactivation on cure: once the past-due amount is paid (the Default is cured), the Company promptly re-enables the Vehicle so it can start normally again.

14.2  Consent. The Renter acknowledges this disclosure and consents to the device on these terms.

What this means
There’s a device that can keep the car from starting again if payment is seriously past due — but it can never shut the car off while you’re driving, and it never touches steering or brakes. You’ll always get a heads-up before it’s used, and the moment you catch up, the car starts again.
Renter initials — starter-interrupt consent

15Default & Remedies

15.1  What counts as Default. Each of the following is a Default: (a) Rent remains unpaid after the grace period in Section 7; (b) the Renter’s insurance lapses, is canceled, or falls below full coverage (Section 9); (c) the Vehicle is used in a prohibited way (Sections 10 and 19); (d) the Renter gives materially false information; or (e) the Renter otherwise materially breaches this Agreement and does not cure within a reasonable time after notice.

15.2  The Company’s remedies, in order. On a Default, the Company may, neutrally and consistently: (1) attempt an off-session charge of the amount due to the payment method on file; (2) after the advance notice described in Section 14, take device action; and (3) recover the Vehicle under Section 16. The Company applies these steps the same way for everyone and only as far as needed to cure the Default.

15.3  Cure. The Renter may stop the ladder at any point by curing the Default — typically by bringing the account current. On cure, normal use resumes (including device reactivation under Section 14).

What this means
If things go wrong — payment stops, insurance drops, the car is misused — here’s exactly what happens, in order, and it’s the same for everyone. Nothing skips ahead, you get notice before any device step, and catching up stops the process at any point.

16Repossession & Personal Property

16.1  Lawful recovery. On a Default, the Company may recover the Vehicle by lawful means and without breach of the peace. The Vehicle remains the Company’s property throughout.

16.2  Your personal property. Personal belongings in a recovered Vehicle remain the Renter’s. The Company will inventory any personal property it finds, store it safely, and make it available for the Renter to retrieve at the Company’s address during normal hours. The Company will not condition the return of personal property on payment of Rent or other amounts.

What this means
If we ever have to take the car back, it’s done lawfully and without confrontation. Anything of yours inside stays yours — we’ll list it, keep it safe, and let you pick it up. We won’t hold your belongings hostage over money.

17Credit Reporting & Collections Disclosure

The Company may report the Renter’s payment performance — whether positive or negative — to consumer credit bureaus, and may place unpaid balances with a third-party collection agency. The Company reports accurately and consistently.

What this means
How you pay can show up on your credit — paying on time can help, and unpaid balances can hurt and may go to collections. We report the truth, nothing more.

18Damage, Normal Wear & Return Condition

18.1  Normal wear is expected. Ordinary wear from normal use — light scuffs, minor interior wear, routine tire and brake wear — is not chargeable.

18.2  Chargeable damage. Damage beyond normal wear — dents, broken glass, torn or burned upholstery, missing equipment, or damage from neglect or misuse — is chargeable. Any charge is measured against the Section 2 Condition Record, so only new damage counts, and is documented for the Renter.

18.3  Return condition. At return, the Vehicle comes back in the same condition as at handoff, normal wear excepted, reasonably clean, with all keys, equipment, and documents, and with a fuel level at least equal to the level at handoff (or the Renter may pay the documented refueling cost).

19Prohibited Uses

19.1  Not allowed. The Renter does not use the Vehicle: for any illegal activity or in furtherance of a crime; for racing, speed contests, or stunts; to tow or push another vehicle or object; off-road or where the Vehicle is not designed to go; or for mechanical alteration of the Vehicle without the Company’s written consent.

19.2  Seizure or impound. If the Vehicle is seized, impounded, or forfeited because of the Renter’s conduct, the Renter bears all resulting liability, cost, and loss, including the Vehicle’s value if it is not recovered.

19.3  Out-of-state use. Out-of-state use is prohibited. The Vehicle must remain within the State of Texas at all times during the Rental Period.

20Indemnification & Limitation of Liability

20.1  Indemnification. Because the Renter controls the operation of the Vehicle, the Renter indemnifies and holds the Company harmless from claims, losses, and costs arising out of the Renter’s or any Authorized Driver’s operation or use of the Vehicle during the Rental Period. This is consistent with the Company’s role as the renting owner under 49 U.S.C. § 30106. It does not extend to loss caused by the Company’s own gross negligence or willful misconduct.

20.2  Limitation of liability. To the extent the law allows, neither Party is liable to the other for indirect, incidental, or consequential damages. Nothing here limits a Party’s liability for its own gross negligence, willful misconduct, or anything that cannot be limited by law.

What this means
Since you’re the one driving, you cover claims that come from your driving — not us. This is the same idea that keeps a rental company from being blamed for how a renter drives. It doesn’t let either of us off the hook for serious, deliberate wrongdoing.

21Dispute Resolution

21.1  Individual arbitration. Any dispute arising out of this Agreement is resolved by binding individual arbitration administered by ARBITRATION PROVIDER under its applicable rules. Disputes are brought on an individual basis, not as a class action.

21.2  Venue and governing law. The arbitration takes place in, and venue lies in, Harris County, Texas, and this Agreement is governed by the laws of the State of Texas. Either Party may still bring an individual claim in small-claims court where it qualifies.

What this means
If we ever truly disagree, we settle it through a neutral arbitrator here in Harris County under Texas law, rather than a long court fight — one-on-one, not as part of a group case. Small everyday claims can still go to small-claims court.
